Open-chord capo positions

A capo raises every open string by one semitone per fret, so a capo on fret 2 turns G shapes into the sound of A. To sound in a key, play the shapes of the key that many semitones lower: the shape key is the sounding key minus the capo fret, counted around the twelve notes. The seven diatonic chords keep their number and quality, so the same I ii iii IV V vi vii° shapes come out in the sounding key; for example, G Am Bm C D Em F#dim played with a capo on fret 2 sounds as A Bm C#m D E F#m G#dim. Roots are spelled with the fewest accidentals, so the shape key for sounding in F with a capo on fret 2 is Eb rather than D#. Open-chord positions list the capo frets that leave familiar open shapes (C, G, D, A, E for major; A, E, D for minor). Partial and cut capos, alternate tunings, barre versus open voicings, dropped tunings and the octave a capo on fret 12 adds are not modelled. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
