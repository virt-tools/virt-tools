One spatial stream carries the data subcarriers of the channel times the bits of the modulation times the coding rate in every OFDM symbol, divided by the symbol time; the link rate multiplies that by the spatial streams. A 20 MHz 802.11n or 802.11ac channel has 52 data subcarriers and a 40 MHz one 108, 802.11ac adds 234 at 80 MHz and 468 at 160 MHz, and 802.11ax uses 234, 468, 980 and 1960. The modulation carries 1 bit per subcarrier at BPSK, 2 at QPSK, 4 at 16-QAM, 6 at 64-QAM, 8 at 256-QAM and 10 at 1024-QAM, with the coding rate of the MCS index. An 802.11n or 802.11ac symbol lasts 4 microseconds plus the guard interval, so MCS 7 at 20 MHz works out at 52 x 6 x 5/6 / 4 us = 65 Mbps; an 802.11ax symbol lasts 12.8 microseconds plus the guard interval, which is 143.4 Mbps at 20 MHz MCS 11 and 600.5 Mbps at 80 MHz. Goodput is the rate with the MAC efficiency you enter, the share of the air that carries payload once preambles, headers, acknowledgement, contention and retries are taken out; a channel-boundary figure of 50 to 70 percent is common, and your own measurements beat any default. Airtime is the stream demand divided by the goodput, and the streams figure divides your airtime budget by that. The rate is the negotiated single-user rate: OFDMA resource units, MU-MIMO, aggregation, short or long guard interval selection, and any vendor-specific extension are not modelled, and 802.11ax MCS 10 and 11 exist only where both ends support them.
