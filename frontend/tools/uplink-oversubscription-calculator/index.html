The oversubscription ratio this boundary should stay within, written as X:1. Access edges are commonly allowed 20:1; aggregation and core links usually sit at 4:1 or below.

Oversubscription is the combined line rate of the access ports divided by the combined line rate of the active uplinks, written X:1. Two uplinks of 10 Gbps behind 48 ports of 1 Gbps is 48 ÷ 20, or 2.4:1, which means all access ports together cannot send at full line rate at once. A ratio above 1 is normal and intended at the access edge because endpoints rarely transmit simultaneously; the peak-utilization figure turns that design ratio into the load the uplinks actually carry, and the headroom is what is left at that load. The ratio says nothing about measured throughput, queueing, latency, per-flow hashing or traffic that stays on the same switch, and a bundle member that is standby or blocked does not add capacity. Values are refused outside 1 to 4096 access ports, 1 to 256 uplink ports, 1% to 100% peak utilization and a 1:1 to 100:1 target, and decimal commas are accepted. This is a planning estimate for a single boundary, it is not a design certification, and it runs entirely in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
