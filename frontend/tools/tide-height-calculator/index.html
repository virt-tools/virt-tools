Tide Height Calculator

Estimate the height of the tide between high and low water. Give the two water heights and how long it has been since the reference water, and the rule of twelfths turns that into the height now, how much the water has already moved and an hour-by-hour curve.

Heights are charted heights in metres and may be negative where the chart datum sits above the lowest water. The interval between the two waters runs from 0 to 6 hours: write 2.5 or 2:30, up to two decimal places.

The rule of twelfths divides the six hours between high and low water into six steps and moves the water by 1, 3, 6, 9, 11 and then 12 twelfths of the tidal range, which is a straight-line approximation to a sine curve. Between whole hours the page joins those steps with straight lines, so an in-between time is interpolated rather than drawn from a real tide curve. The rule assumes a semi-diurnal tide that rises and falls in about six hours; mixed and diurnal tides, shallow estuaries, river flow, wind and barometric pressure all move real water away from it. Treat the result as a planning estimate and use the official tide tables, harmonic predictions and local knowledge for navigation, anchoring and drying heights.
