How the sizing is worked out. The EPA sizing chart pairs a floor area with a minimum particle CADR at an 8 ft (2.4 m) ceiling: 65 cfm for 100 ft² up to 390 cfm for 600 ft², which is 0.65 cfm per square foot. Because the chart is tied to that ceiling height, the page scales the rate in proportion to room volume, so a taller room asks for proportionally more clean air. The equivalent air changes per hour are the CADR divided by the room volume, and the clearance times come from a well-mixed single-room model in which the concentration falls exponentially, so removing 90% takes ln 10 and removing 99% takes ln 100 room volumes of clean air. The largest room figure inverts the same rule at the ceiling height you entered. These are estimates for one room: the model assumes the cleaner runs at its rated speed, the air is well mixed, and no new particles arrive while it runs. CADR is rated for particles only and is usually measured at the highest fan speed, gases and odours need an activated-carbon filter, and no air cleaner replaces source control and ventilation.
