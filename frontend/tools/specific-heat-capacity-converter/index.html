Specific Heat Capacity Converter

Convert a specific heat capacity — the heat a material stores per kilogram and per kelvin — between SI and the calorie, Btu and kilowatt-hour units that handbooks, older drawings and food labels still use. International Table and thermochemical calorie values are listed separately, because they differ.

A specific heat capacity is an intensive property: multiply it by a mass and a temperature difference to get an energy, in joules, calories or Btus. Water near room temperature is 4184 J/(kg·K), which is why one thermochemical calorie per gram-degree Celsius, 4184 J/(kg·K), and one International Table Btu per pound-degree Fahrenheit, 4186.8 J/(kg·K), are within 0.07 % of each other but not equal. The International Table calorie is exactly 4.1868 J and the thermochemical calorie exactly 4.184 J; the International Table Btu is exactly 1055.05585262 J, a pound is 0.45359237 kg and a Fahrenheit degree interval is 5/9 K. A kilowatt-hour is exactly 3.6 MJ. Both the joule-per-kilogram and the joule-per-kilogram-kelvin are accepted, because a temperature interval of one kelvin is one degree Celsius. Results are rounded to six significant digits; nothing is sent or stored.
