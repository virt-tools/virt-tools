Brewhouse Water Planner

Work back from the beer you want. Enter the volume into the fermenter, the grain bill, the mash thickness and your kettle's losses, and see the strike water, sparge water and total water to heat.

Boil-off, kettle loss and absorption belong to your equipment, not to the recipe. Measure them once and they hold for every batch; the defaults are starting points.

The plan runs backwards from the fermenter, because that is the volume you are trying to hit. The wort needed at flameout, chilled, is the batch plus the kettle loss; hot, it is 4 % larger, because wort contracts as it cools from boiling to pitching temperature. Adding the boil-off gives the pre-boil volume, and adding what the grain keeps gives the water to heat. Strike water is the grain bill times the mash thickness; sparge water is whatever is left of the total. Absorption is set at 1 L/kg, near the middle of the 0.8–1.1 L/kg that published brewing guides quote for a drained mash, and it is the number most worth measuring on your own system: a squeezed brew bag holds back less, a deep grain bed more. Mash-tun dead space below the outlet is not modelled separately — fold it into the kettle loss if your tun traps wort. The page plans volumes only: it does not predict gravity, efficiency, mash pH or fermentation, and it makes no food-safety claim. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
