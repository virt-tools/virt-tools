Mechanical specific energy is the mechanical work done per unit volume of rock destroyed. Teale's form is MSE = WOB / A + 120π · RPM · T / (A · ROP), with weight in pounds, torque in foot-pounds, bit face area in square inches and penetration rate in feet per hour, which gives psi; the same constant 120π works in SI units with newtons, newton-metres, square metres and metres per hour, which gives pascals. The first term is the bit pressing into the rock, the second is the torque turning it, and at normal rates the second carries almost all of the energy. The figure is only as good as its inputs: surface torque includes the friction of the whole drill string, so a torque measured at the bit is better, and the penetration rate should be averaged over a steady interval rather than read at one instant. It is a monitoring number for one hole size and one bit; it does not predict the rate of penetration and does not measure rock strength unless it is compared with a strength measured in the same formation.
