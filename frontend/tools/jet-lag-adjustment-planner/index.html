How the numbers work

  • Clock change. Destination offset minus origin offset, kept as a signed number of hours. New York (−5) to Tokyo (+9) is +14h: the wall clock jumps forward 14 hours.
  • Body-clock shift. The body adjusts in one direction only, so the planner takes the shorter way around the clock: a 14-hour jump forward is planned as a 10-hour shift later, and 10 hours forward is 14 hours earlier. The sign says whether bedtime moves earlier (advance) or later (delay).
  • Daily shift. The needed shift divided by the days available, capped at the maximum you allow. If the cap binds, full adjustment finishes after more days than you entered, and the result says so.
  • Arrival bedtime. Your habitual bedtime is shifted by the full body-clock amount and shown in destination local time. Waking time keeps the same sleep length, so the sleep window stays intact while it moves.
  • Schedule. Day 0 is arrival day and applies the whole body-clock offset; each later day moves bedtime one daily step toward your habitual local bedtime. Bedtime can fall at any hour while the body clock is still misaligned.
  • This is time arithmetic for planning, not medical advice. Individual sleep patterns vary, and light, meals, naps and travel wear all affect how a trip feels. The page performs no storage, uploads or network requests.
