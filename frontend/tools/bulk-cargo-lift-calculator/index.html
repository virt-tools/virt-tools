CargoStowage factorLiftBinds

The lift is the smaller of two numbers: the tonnes the hold volume can take at the cargo's stowage factor (grain capacity divided by the stowage factor) and the deadweight cargo capacity. When the holds fill first the ship is space-limited and deadweight is left unused; when the deadweight runs out first the ship is weight-limited and hold space is left empty. Cargo volume is the lift times the stowage factor, and hold use is that volume as a percentage of the grain capacity. The stowage factor used here is the volume one tonne occupies in the hold, including the normal stowage losses described in the reference below, so no separate broken-stowage allowance is added on top. Grain capacity is 1 to 2,000,000 m³, deadweight cargo capacity 1 to 1,000,000 t and the stowage factor 0.05 to 20 m³/t. This is a planning estimate only, not a loading instruction: the final figure comes from the ship's stability booklet, load line and cargo declaration, and from the terminal's measured weights.
