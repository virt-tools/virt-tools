A band saw blade is a loop around two wheels: it runs straight between them twice and wraps half of each wheel, so the length is twice the axle spacing plus half the circumference of each wheel, with a small correction of (D1 − D2)² ÷ 4C when the wheels are different sizes. Measure each wheel across the tyre, not the casting, and take the axle spacing with the tensioner at the middle of its travel, because moving the upper wheel changes the blade length by about twice that movement. That is why a saw takes a band of lengths rather than one figure: owners’ manuals usually list a stock length inside the band, and a blade near the middle of the travel leaves room to take up stretch and to slacken it for storage. Lengths are also given to the nearest 1/8 in, which is how most suppliers weld them. The calculator assumes a two-wheel saw with plain tyres and does not model three-wheel saws, crowned or stepped tyres, blade thickness, wheel tilt or a tensioner with limited travel, so check the answer against the saw’s manual before ordering.
