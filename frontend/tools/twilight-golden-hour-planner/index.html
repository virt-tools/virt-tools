Times are local clock times for the UTC offset you enter, morning first and evening second. Twilight is named by how far the sun sits below the horizon: civil at 6°, nautical at 12° and astronomical at 18°. The blue hour runs from 6° to 4° below and the golden hour from 4° below up to 6° above, so golden light starts before sunrise and lasts past sunset. Sunrise and sunset use the standard 0.833° horizon dip for refraction and the sun's disc. The solar position follows the NOAA solar equations for the date at 00:00 UTC, which are usually within a minute or two but neglect your elevation, terrain, atmospheric pressure and temperature. Where the sun never reaches the needed altitude, the row says so instead of inventing a time; at high latitudes that is a real midnight-sun or polar-night result. Daylight saving time is not applied, so use the offset in force on that date. Everything is calculated in the browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
