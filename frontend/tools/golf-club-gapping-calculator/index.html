    How the gaps are worked out

    • Sort, then subtract. The clubs are ordered by carry, longest first, and each gap is the difference between a club and the one below it. The gaps always add up to the span from the longest club to the shortest.
    • What counts as a hole. A gap wider than 15 yd (13.7 m) is flagged as wide: there is no club for that distance, so one shot in the set has to be manufactured. A gap under 8 yd (7.3 m) is flagged as tight, because two clubs do the same job; under 3 yd (2.7 m) they are twins and one is dead weight.
    • The even spread. The average gap is the span divided by the number of intervals, which is one fewer than the number of clubs. The ideal carry for the k-th club below the longest is the longest minus k average gaps, so a set that follows the ideal exactly has every club on a step of its own.
    • Why the average is not a target on its own. Adding a club shortens every interval but does not fix a hole: a set can average 12 yards and still leave a 30-yard hole between the driver and the longest iron. Read the gap column, not just the average.
    • Carry, not total. Total distance adds roll, which changes with the fairway, the wind and the launch, so it cannot be compared across clubs. Use the carry you can repeat: a well-struck average, not your best one.
    • What it does not do. No launch-monitor fitting, no shot dispersion or offline pattern, no wind or altitude adjustment, no club recommendation for a particular course, and no gapping between wedge lofts inside a single club. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
