Magnetic Declination Calculator

Find the angle from true north to magnetic north at a place and date, and the correction to apply to a compass bearing.

Decimal degrees, with an optional N/S/E/W suffix. Elevation is height above sea level; the model measures from the WGS84 ellipsoid, and the difference moves the answer by less than 0.01°.

Declination is positive east: a value of 1.3° E means magnetic north sits 1.3° east of true north, so a magnetic bearing plus the declination gives the true bearing. This page evaluates the World Magnetic Model 2025 (WMM2025), the degree-12 spherical-harmonic model that NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information and the British Geological Survey produce with the US National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency and the UK Defence Geographic Centre. Its coefficients are tied to the epoch 2025.0 and carry annual secular-variation terms that this page applies linearly, which is why the model is valid from 2025.0 to 2030.0 and replaced every five years; outside that window the form refuses the date rather than extrapolating. The elevation is read as height above the WGS84 ellipsoid, the model's own vertical datum, and a sea-level height is used unchanged because the geoid offset shifts the answer by a few hundredths of a degree. Declination is undefined at the geographic poles and changes fastest near the magnetic poles, so latitudes within a thousandth of a degree of ±90° are refused. The model describes the main field only: steel in a boat or a building, local ore bodies and a compass's own deviation card are not in it. No network request is made, and nothing is stored.
