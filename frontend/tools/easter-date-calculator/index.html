Easter is the Sunday after the ecclesiastical full moon that falls on or after 21 March. The Western churches use the Gregorian calendar and the tables of Dionysius Exiguus; the Orthodox churches keep the Julian calendar for the paschal full moon and then convert the result to the civil calendar. Both computus rules are pure arithmetic on the year, so no moon observation, location or timezone is involved. The four-digit range starts at 1583, the first year after the Gregorian reform of 1582, and the Julian to Gregorian offset is the number of days the Julian calendar trails the civil one in that year. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
