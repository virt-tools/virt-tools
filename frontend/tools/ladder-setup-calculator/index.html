OSHA's construction rule for a non-self-supporting ladder is that the foot stands back a distance equal to a quarter of the working length, where the working length is the distance along the ladder from the foot to the top support (29 CFR 1926.1053(b)(5)(i)). Written as geometry, a quarter set-back fixes the lean at 75.5° from horizontal, which is the same 75½° the standard names when it load-tests a portable ladder. For a given support height h that gives a working length of h × 4/√15 and a set-back of h/√15, so the two figures always move together: a taller climb needs a longer ladder and a larger set-back, never one without the other. A ladder used to reach an upper landing also has to stand at least 3 ft (0.9 m) above that landing (1926.1053(b)(1)); where the ladder's length will not allow it, the rule asks for the top to be secured to a rigid support and a grasping device such as a grab rail to be provided. The rule covers leaning ladders only: a stepladder is self-supporting, is used fully open, and its top step is never used as a step (1926.1053(b)(13)). This page is geometry. It does not check the ladder's duty rating or condition, the ground and its firmness, the tie-off, overhead power lines, or how the ladder is footed, and it does not decide whether a ladder is the right access for the job.
