H.264 splits capabilities into levels, and a decoder that supports a level must decode any stream inside that level's limits. The frame is measured in 16×16 macroblocks: the width and height are divided by 16 and rounded up, their product is the picture size in macroblocks, and that product times the frame rate is the macroblock rate. A level carries the stream when the picture fits its maximum frame size, neither side exceeds √(8 × that size), and the macroblock rate is within its maximum. The bitrate ceiling is the level's MaxBR in Table A-1 multiplied by the profile's Table A-2 factor: 1.2 for Baseline, Main and Extended, 1.5 for High, 3.6 for High 10 and 4.8 for the two 4:4:4 profiles. The DPB figure is MaxDpbMbs divided by the picture size, capped at 16, which is how many reference frames the decoder must hold. Levels are read from ITU-T H.264 Table A-1 and the profile factors from Table A-2. Level 1b is left out because it only applies to constrained-baseline streams, and VBV buffer size, the MinCR bitrate floor, interlaced field coding and the level-signalling syntax itself are not checked. A level is a decoder capability label, not a picture-quality setting.
