In a constant-voltage system every amplifier of a given line voltage puts out the same maximum RMS voltage, 70.7 V being the common US standard and 100 V the common European one, and each loudspeaker carries a transformer whose primary tap is marked in watts at that voltage. Because the wattages are all quoted at the same voltage they add like power, so the total load is simply the sum of the taps and the line impedance the amplifier sees is Z = V squared / P, with a line current of I = P / V. A 70.7 V line therefore presents about 5,000 / P ohms, which is why the same 100 W tap is a very different load on a 25 V line. Each doubling of tap power adds 3.01 dB, so the page also shows every tap against the 1 W tap as 10 log10 of the tap in watts. The amplifier's rating must be quoted at the line voltage you select, and the headroom allowance covers transformer insertion loss, tap tolerance, long cable runs and program peaks; the 20% default is a planning habit, not part of the standard. This is a budget, not a measurement: it ignores cable loss, transformer saturation, impedance minima with frequency and loudspeaker sensitivity, and it does not apply to low-impedance 4, 8 or 16 ohm loads.
