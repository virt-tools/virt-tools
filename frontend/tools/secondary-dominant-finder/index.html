A secondary dominant is the dominant seventh of a chord other than the tonic, written V7/x: it is a perfect fifth above the chord it resolves to, and its third is that chord's root raised by a semitone, so it pulls down onto it. Its root, third, fifth and seventh are stacked as thirds, which fixes the spelling. Borrowed chords come from the parallel mode: in a major key, the minor iv, the flat degrees ♭VI, ♭VII, ♭III and the Neapolitan ♭II; in a minor key, the major IV and V and the Picardy I. Only diatonic targets are matched, so a chromatic chord is not itself used as the target of an applied dominant, the leading-tone chords (viiŶ/x) and tritone substitutions are not listed, and the melodic and harmonic minor scales are treated as borrowings rather than as the key. Flat-side keys therefore produce spellings such as C♭ major or F♭ major for the far borrowed chords: they are correct for the degree but uncommon in practice.
