Uses the classic home roux rule of one, two or three level tablespoons of flour per US cup of liquid for a thin soup, a medium sauce and a thick gravy. A US cup is 236.588 mL and a level tablespoon of all-purpose flour is 8 g, so the three settings work out to about 3.4%, 6.8% and 10.1% flour by weight in a water-like liquid. Butter is measured to match the flour's volume (1 tbsp = 14.2 g), the usual kitchen ratio, which is about 1.8 times the flour's weight. A roux carries no salt, so season the sauce separately, and flour keeps thickening as it simmers and cools — add liquid rather than more flour if it overshoots. The page does not cover cornstarch slurries, reductions or other thickeners, and it is a cooking estimate rather than food-safety guidance.
