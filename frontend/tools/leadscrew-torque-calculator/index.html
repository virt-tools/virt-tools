One turn of the thread unwraps into a right triangle with a base of πdm and a height equal to the lead l = starts × pitch, which is the lead angle λ = arctan(l / πdm). Resolving the load and the friction along that incline gives the torque to raise T = (F dm / 2)(l + πμdmsecα) / (πdm − μl secα) and to lower T = (F dm / 2)(πμdmsecα − l) / (πdm + μl secα), with α the half thread angle and secα carrying the wedging of the thread flanks. The collar adds Tc = Fμcdc / 2, and the overall efficiency is the work delivered F l over the work put in 2πT. A screw self-locks when μsecα ≥ tanλ, so the lower torque is zero or negative and the load stays put without a brake. The mean diameter is d − 0.6495P for a 60° thread and d − 0.5P for an Acme or square thread. This is a steady, uniform-load friction model: it ignores thread and nut stresses, buckling, wear, running-in, vibration that can drop the effective friction and let a nominally self-locking screw back-drive, starting torque and the critical speed, so treat it as a first sizing estimate, not a design review.
