journald Storage Budget Planner

Turn a filesystem size, a free-space figure and a daily log volume into the [Journal] size limits for systemd-journald, and see how many days of logs they hold.

Persistent storage keeps the journal in /var/log/journal, volatile storage in /run/log/journal. Leave the size limit blank to use journald’s own default: 10 % of the filesystem, capped at 4 GiB. For volatile storage the filesystem is the size of /run, usually half of RAM.

The defaults are 10 % for the size limit and 15 % for the KeepFree reserve, each capped at 4 GiB; journald honours both and uses the smaller, so a filesystem can be too full for the journal to reach its limit. The largest file defaults to one eighth of the limit, capped at 128 MiB, which is why a 4 GiB budget is kept in 32 files. Only archived files are deleted when journald vacuums, so a journal can sit above its limit until the active file rotates, and compression is on by default, so the days printed here are a floor rather than a ceiling. Sizes are written to whole MiB. systemd-journald reads this file at start-up, so a change takes effect on a restart of the service. Everything is computed in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere else.
