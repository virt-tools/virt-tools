Ganache is a mass balance: the ratio is chocolate to cream by weight, so chocolate grams are the ratio times the cream grams. Added liquid — liqueur, or a fruit purée in place of some cream — counts on the liquid side and takes the same share of chocolate, which is the usual allowance of 100 g of chocolate per 100 g of liqueur at 1:1. A 2:1 dark-chocolate ganache, a 1:1 icing, and a 2.5:1 milk or white ganache follow the common ratios; firmness also depends on how much cocoa butter and water the chocolate brings, so a ganache can still need adjusting by a little chocolate or cream. Weights are grams, rounded to 0.1 g, and the total is the sum of the rounded parts. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
