HarmonicWrittenSoundsCents from ET

A valved brass instrument plays the harmonic series of its open tube, and each valve adds a length of tubing that lowers the whole series. The traditional layout is tuned in just intonation: the first valve is 1/8 of the main tube, the second 1/15 and the third 1/5, so the valves lower the series by 9:8 (203.9 cents), 16:15 (111.7 cents) and 6:5 (315.6 cents) and a combination adds the lengths rather than the intervals. That is why 1-2 (303.6 cents) is not the same note as 3 (315.6 cents), why 2-3 comes to 409.2 cents instead of 400, and why 1-3 and 1-2-3 are sharp of a true fourth and tritone and need the third valve slide pulled or a trigger used. Each harmonic is that many times the open pedal, so a fingering can sound the pedal multiplied by 2 through 8, and the page keeps every combination whose pitch lands within 50 cents of the note you typed. Everything is in written pitch: the written pedal is C3 on all four instruments, so the series runs from written F#3 to C6 on each of them, and the instrument choice changes only what the note sounds like, because a Bb instrument sounds a major second below its written pitch and the F horn a perfect fifth below. Cents are measured against 12-tone equal temperament with A4 at 440 Hz, so a negative figure is flat; the harmonics themselves are not equal-tempered, which is why the open fifth harmonic reads 13.7 cents flat and the seventh 31.2 cents flat. Two rows can show the same pitch when a valve lands a higher harmonic on a lower one, as the third valve on the sixth harmonic is the fifth. This is the plain three-valve layout only: no fourth valve, compensated tubing, triggers, slides or hand-stopping, no alternative crooks or instrument keys, and no claim about which fingering a chart or a player prefers, since that depends on the passage, the slide pulls and the player's ear. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network requests.
