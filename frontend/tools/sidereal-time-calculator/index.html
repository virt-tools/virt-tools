Sidereal Time Calculator

Turn a UTC instant into sidereal time — the right ascension standing on a meridian — and the hour angle of whatever you are about to observe.

Longitude 0 is the Greenwich meridian; west longitudes are negative. Add a right ascension to get its hour angle and when it transits.

Sidereal time is the hour angle of the vernal equinox, so the local figure is the right ascension on your meridian and objects with that right ascension are at their highest. A mean sidereal day is 23 h 56 min 4.0905 s of civil time, which is why a star transits about 3 min 56 s earlier each night. Mean values ignore nutation; the apparent values add the equation of the equinoxes, at most about 1.2 s. The input is UTC rather than UT1, which can differ by up to 0.9 s, so read the seconds as good to about a second: the U.S. Naval Observatory quotes 0.432 s maximum error for 2000–2100 when the algorithm is fed UT1. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
