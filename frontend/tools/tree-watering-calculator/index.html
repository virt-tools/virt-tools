How the soak is worked out. The rule of thumb is ten US gallons of water for every inch of trunk diameter, measured at knee height, applied slowly within the drip line so it sinks in rather than running off. The page keeps that as a volume per soak and multiplies by the number of soaks you plan each week; water once a week through summer and the two numbers are the same. Bucket and watering-can counts are rounded up, because a part-filled container still counts as one to carry, and hose time is the soak volume divided by the flow you enter. The rule is a starting point for established trees: newly planted trees want smaller, more frequent soakings, sandy soil holds less water than clay, and a hot windy week dries the ground faster. Split a soak into two passes if water starts to pool, and stop short of the trunk. Everything is calculated in the browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
