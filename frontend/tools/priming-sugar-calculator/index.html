    How the amount is worked out

    • Fermentation left CO2 behind. The beer already holds roughly 3.0378 − 0.050062·T + 0.00026555·T² volumes of CO2, with T in °F, so warmer beer holds less and needs more sugar to reach the same target. Enter the warmest temperature the beer reached after fermentation, not the temperature on bottling day.
    • The sugar only supplies the difference. One volume of CO2 is 1.96 g per litre, and a gram of sugar yields a fixed share of its mass as CO2: 0.514 g for sucrose, 0.444 g for dextrose monohydrate (the retail "corn sugar", 9.1 % water of hydration) and 0.489 g for anhydrous dextrose, all from the balanced fermentation equation.
    • Charts differ. Priming calculators assume different sugar yields, so published figures for corn sugar can differ from these by several per cent. The constants here are the stoichiometric ones, and they reproduce the published 0.86 volumes at 68 °F and about 128 g of corn sugar for 5 US gallons at 2.4 volumes.
    • Dissolve the sugar in a little boiled water, cool it, and mix it into the whole batch before bottling. Over 3 volumes needs heavy bottles; the tool gives amounts but no brewing or safety advice. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
