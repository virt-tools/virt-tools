Screenplay Page & Scene Breakdown

Paste a script to count its pages in eighths and see how its scenes split between interior and exterior, day and night.

A formatted page holds about 55 lines in 12-point Courier.

Pages are also reported in eighths, the unit a scheduling board uses: 1 1/2 pages is 12 eighths.

Scene headings are read as they are written: INT. or EXT., the location, then the time of day after a spaced hyphen. Line breaks and blank lines count towards the page estimate.

A screenplay page is a fixed grid rather than a fixed amount of prose: 12-point Courier, a 1-inch top and bottom margin and about 55 lines to the page, whatever the paper size, which is why crews measure scripts in pages and eighths of a page. This page counts the lines of what you paste, ignoring blank lines at the very start and end, divides by the lines per page you enter, and rounds the result up to the next whole eighth, so 55 lines is one page and eight eighths, 56 lines is 1 1/8 pages, and a partial eighth is never dropped. A scene heading is a line that begins with INT, EXT, INT./EXT or I/E, optionally after a scene number such as 12A; the text after the last spaced hyphen is the time of day, counted as day when it is exactly DAY, as night when it is exactly NIGHT, and as other for DAWN, DUSK, CONTINUOUS, LATER or anything else, after dropping a parenthetical such as (CONTINUOUS). Headings written without the spaced hyphen, and headings whose time of day is blank, are reported as having no time of day. The estimate assumes the paste keeps the script's own line breaks: a PDF copy that reflows paragraphs, a draft in a proportional font, or a file whose page breaks were dropped will all count differently, and the page count says nothing about running time, which depends on the direction. No network request is made, and nothing is stored.
