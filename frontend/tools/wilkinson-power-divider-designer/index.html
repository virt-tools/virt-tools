Each arm is a quarter-wave line: 90° of electrical length at the design frequency, with a characteristic impedance that transforms the two output ports into the system impedance at the input. For an equal split both arms are 1.414 times the system impedance and the resistor between the outputs is twice the system impedance. For an unequal split the power ratio K² = P3/P2 sets the arms at Z0√(K(1+K²)) and Z0√((1+K²)/K³), and the resistor at Z0(K + 1/K); at K = 1 those fall back to the equal-split values. The physical length is a quarter of the guided wavelength, vf × c / f, so it scales with the velocity factor of the line you build. Both outputs are in phase and isolated from each other by the resistor, which dissipates the reflected power when one output is mismatched. The page assumes an ideal lossless line, exactly 90° at the design frequency, a matched source and matched loads, and it does not model dispersion, junction capacitance, resistor tolerance or how far the arms are from the ground plane; at other frequencies the match and isolation degrade.
