DCP Delivery Planner

Size a digital cinema package from its runtime and picture settings: frame count, frame budget, picture and sound data, and the drives it needs.

Feature length as h:mm:ss, or mm:ss for a short: 2 hours is 2:00:00.

The DCI container, not the picture: 2K allows 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 and 60 fps, 4K only 24, 25 and 30 fps.

The active picture sits inside the container, so flat and HDTV get pillarbox bars and scope gets letterbox bars.

DCI frame rates are whole numbers: 23.976 is delivered as 24 in a DCP.

The encoder's target for the JPEG 2000 picture, in megabits per second. The DCI ceiling is 250 Mbit/s.

Up to 16 channels of uncompressed 24-bit PCM sound.

48 kHz is the usual cinema rate; 96 kHz doubles the sound data.

Usable capacity of the CRU-style drive the package ships on, in decimal gigabytes.

A digital cinema package is not a video file: the picture is a JPEG 2000 track of full frames in CIE XYZ at 12 bits per component, the sound is uncompressed 24-bit PCM, and both are wrapped in MXF with a composition playlist and packing list, so a player ingests the whole package before the first frame. This page budgets that package. The frame count is the runtime times the frame rate, and every size is computed from that frame count, so a runtime quoted to the second lands on a whole frame at every rate here and the page also prints the count as a timecode. The picture size is the frame count times the per-frame budget, bit rate ÷ 8 ÷ frame rate, and it is the encoder's target rather than a promise: JPEG 2000 is variable rate, so the finished track lands near it. The sound size is channels × sample rate × 3 bytes × the presentation time, which is why 5.1 at 48 kHz costs about 6.9 Mbit/s. A 1% allowance covers the MXF wrapping, the playlist and packing list and their hashes; subtitles, encryption and the extra reels of a long feature are not modelled, and sizes are decimal gigabytes, the way drives are sold. The DCI limits are the reason the page refuses some combinations: the image essence is capped at 250 Mbit/s, a 4K container carries only 24, 25 and 30 fps, and a stereoscopic package is 2K at 24 or 48 fps with both eyes sharing the 250 Mbit/s, so each eye gets at most 125. The container is always 2048 × 1080 or 4096 × 2160; flat, scope, HDTV and full are the active pictures inside it, and the page reports the bars that fills. Everything runs in your browser with no uploads, storage or network requests, and the page plans a deliverable rather than encoding one.
