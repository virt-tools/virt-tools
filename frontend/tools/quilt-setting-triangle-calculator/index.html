A block set on point is as wide as its diagonal, the finished size times the square root of 2. The side triangles that fill the gaps between the block points along each edge are quarter-square triangles: cut a square of the block diagonal plus five seam allowances, twice on the diagonal, and each square yields four. The four corners are half-square triangles from a square of the diagonal divided by the root of two, plus three and a half seam allowances, cut once on the diagonal for two. Five and three and a half allowances give the 1 1/4 in and 7/8 in that quilting references add at a 1/4 in seam. Sizes are rounded up to the next 1/8 in, or to the next whole millimetre. Cutting the side squares twice leaves the straight grain on the long outside edge, where a bias edge would stretch, and the corner squares once puts it on the short edges at the corners. The layout needs two side triangles per gap along the top and bottom and down each side, so 2 x (across - 1) + 2 x (down - 1), and always four corners. Sashing, borders, backing and binding are not included, and the finished centre is measured to the block points before any border.
