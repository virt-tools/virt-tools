Thermal Expansion Coefficient Converter

Convert a coefficient of linear thermal expansion — the fractional change in length per kelvin — between reciprocal kelvin, reciprocal degree Fahrenheit, parts per million and micro-inch per inch-degree Fahrenheit units. A coefficient quoted per degree Fahrenheit is 1.8 times the same coefficient per kelvin, because a Fahrenheit degree interval is 5/9 of a kelvin.

The linear coefficient of thermal expansion, usually written α, is the fractional change in length per kelvin: a length L changes by α L ΔT when the temperature moves by ΔT. It is a property of the material, quoted in reciprocal kelvin or in one of the units above, and it is not a strain, a stress or a movement allowance by itself. A reciprocal kelvin and a reciprocal degree Celsius carry the same factor, because a temperature interval of one kelvin is one degree Celsius; a reciprocal degree Fahrenheit is 9/5 per kelvin, since a Fahrenheit degree interval is 5/9 K. A part per million per kelvin is 10−6/K, which is the same as a micrometre per metre-kelvin because one micrometre is 10−6 m, and a micro-inch per inch-degree Fahrenheit is 10−6/°F, so it equals 1.8 × 10−6/K. Negative coefficients are accepted, because some materials — zirconium tungstate, and water between 0 °C and 4 °C — contract as they warm. A conversion multiplies by the source factor and divides by the target factor, so every pair round trips. Input must be a plain decimal, optionally grouped in thousands; a leading decimal point is accepted, exponent form is refused, and a magnitude of 10 per kelvin or more is rejected with the reason. Results carry six significant digits with trailing zeros trimmed and thousands grouped. The reference table holds round coefficients in two systems, and the output also states the change of a one-metre length per kelvin in micrometres. Coefficients vary with temperature and between alloys, so a handbook figure is only as good as its stated range. Everything runs in the browser; nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
