What each lockout duration buys

The entered threshold and attempt rate against one account, at the lockout durations a policy usually chooses from. The entered duration is shown in bold.
LockoutCycleGuesses per hourSlowdown

The attacker works in cycles. While the account is unlocked it spends the threshold divided by its attempt rate to burn the allowed failures, then waits out the lockout, so one cycle lasts that time plus the lockout duration and yields exactly the threshold in guesses. Sustained guesses per hour is the threshold times 60 divided by the cycle, the slowdown is the unthrottled rate divided by that figure, and the locked-out share of the cycle is the duration over the cycle. A longer lockout or a lower threshold cuts the rate; a faster attacker gains almost nothing, because the wait, not the attempt time, sets the pace. The model treats each lockout as a fresh allowance, which is exact when the duration is at least the counting window: the failures have aged out by the time the account unlocks. When the duration is shorter than the window, some failures are usually still counted at unlock, so the attacker gets fewer than the threshold and the result is an upper bound — the page says so. Distributed attempts, per-IP counters, CAPTCHA and bot detection, progressive delays that grow with each failure, and shared or single sign-on identities are not modelled, and spreading the same attempts over many accounts is handled only by the accounts field. Nothing is uploaded, stored or fetched — the page runs entirely in your browser.
