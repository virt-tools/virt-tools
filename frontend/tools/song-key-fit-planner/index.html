How the fit is chosen

  • One shift for the whole melody. A transposition moves every note of the song by the same number of semitones, so the melody keeps its shape and the span between its lowest and highest note never changes.
  • A fit means both ends are covered. The melody only counts as fitting when its lowest note is at or above the voice's lowest comfortable note and its highest note is at or below the top. If the melody is wider than the voice, no transposition can fit and the page says so.
  • As written first, then centring. If the melody already fits the voice, no transposition is recommended and the other keys that fit are listed below it. Otherwise the recommended shift is the one that leaves the most room at the closer end, so the melody sits as near the middle of the voice as it can; a shift of zero wins any tie.

Notes are read as scientific pitch, where middle C is C4 and A4 is the 440 Hz reference, and transposed names are spelled to suit the key they land in, with flats in flat keys and sharps elsewhere. Key names follow the conventional twelve spellings of each mode, so a shift up from C major reads as Db major rather than C# major. This is range arithmetic only: shifts of up to an octave in either direction are listed. Tessitura, stamina, passaggio, breath, ornamentation and voice leading are not modelled, and a comfortable low on one day may not be a comfortable low on another, so let the singer decide.
