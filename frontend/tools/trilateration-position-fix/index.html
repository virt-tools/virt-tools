Enter three stations and their distances to see the fix.

Each distance is a great-circle distance on a sphere of mean radius 6,371 km, so the three circles around the stations meet at the fitted point. Real measurements rarely agree exactly: the page reports the least-squares point and refuses when the best fit is worse than 1 km root mean square, which usually means a distance belongs to another station or a unit is mixed. Three ranges leave no redundancy, so the result carries no error estimate of its own; the geometry factor shows how much a distance error is magnified (about 1.15 is ideal when three stations are evenly spaced around the point, and larger values are weaker). If the three stations lie nearly in a line, the same distances also fit a position mirrored across that line, so the page declines that arrangement instead of picking a side. A radio, GNSS or survey fix needs more ranges and a proper error model. This is a planning and cross-check aid, not a navigation system. Nothing is stored and the page makes no network requests.
