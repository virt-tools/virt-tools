    What this does not do

    • This is a materials count, not a design. It says nothing about whether a sandbag wall will hold back water at your site, and a temporary barrier of this kind can fail or be overtopped. Follow local flood-fighting guidance and emergency instructions instead of relying on this page.
    • The wall is counted as one bag thick, with bags laid end to end along the run and courses stacked on top of each other. A wall taller than a few courses is normally built with a wider base or a pyramid section, which needs more bags than the count shown.
    • Filled bag sizes and weights are typical trade figures, not a specification for any one product. Bags that arrive flat, torn or overfilled change the count, and the sand volume assumes dry sand at about 100 lb per cubic foot.

    Building order

    • Lay a plastic sheet along the run so water cannot seep under the wall.
    • Fill bags about two thirds full, so they flatten and bond when stacked.
    • Lay the first course end to end, with the bag necks tucked under.
    • Offset every course by half a bag, so joints never line up.
    • Press each bag into the one below, and keep the face of the wall vertical.
    • Keep the spare bags dry and covered, and stop work if water starts to run past the ends.
