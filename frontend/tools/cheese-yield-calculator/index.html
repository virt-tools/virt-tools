Cheese Yield Calculator

Predict the cheese a vat of milk gives from its fat and casein, and the milk each kilogram of cheese takes. The page applies the Van Slyke yield formula, reports the yield per 100 kg of milk and the weight from the whole vat. Everything is computed locally.

Cheddar and Colby are the varieties the formula was fitted to; 37 percent is a typical Cheddar moisture, part-skim mozzarella is nearer 48. The protein figure is used only for an estimate.

The Van Slyke and Price formula predicts cheese yield from the fat and casein of the milk and the moisture the cheese is made to: yield per 100 kg of milk is (0.93 × fat + casein − 0.1) × 1.09 ÷ (1 − moisture). The 0.93 keeps 93 percent of the milk fat in the cheese, the 0.1 is the casein lost in the whey as glycomacropeptide, and the 1.09 accounts for the salt, milk salts and lactose that stay in the curd, about nine percent of its fat and casein. Casein is usually estimated from the total protein, which the analyzer reports; this page defaults to the common casein number of 0.78, and offers the 0.830 × protein − 0.199 regression from the 1998 Ontario bulk-tank survey, or you can type the casein figure from a casein-calibrated test. The result is a theoretical target for rennet-set varieties, not a promise: pasteurisation heat, whey protein in the vat, draining pH, curd fines and salt all move the real yield by a few percent.
