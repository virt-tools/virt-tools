Enter the two span edges and the resolution bandwidth to see the sweep time.

For a near-Gaussian analog resolution filter the sweep time is k × span / RBW², so narrowing the RBW by ten multiplies the sweep time by a hundred. k is the ratio RBW² divided by the sweep rate, and a k of 2 to 3 covers the Gaussian filters that swept spectrum analyzers were built around: below 2 the filter cannot settle as the sweep passes and the display goes uncalibrated, above 3 the sweep simply waits longer than the span and RBW need. The span is the stop frequency minus the start frequency, and the resolution elements across it are the span divided by the RBW. With a time budget the widest usable RBW is the square root of k × span divided by that time, and the rate at that RBW is the span divided by the budget. Start and stop frequencies from 0 to 1 THz with the stop above the start, an RBW above 0 and at most 1 GHz, k from 1 to 10 and an optional budget above 0 and at most 1 000 000 s are accepted; text, exponent notation, a blank required field and out-of-range values are refused with the reason, and decimal commas work. This is the formula for a swept analog analyzer: it does not know your instrument’s own sweep-time limit, its FFT or oversweep modes, or its error at a given rate, so compare the result with the instrument’s manual and its uncalibrated-display warning.
