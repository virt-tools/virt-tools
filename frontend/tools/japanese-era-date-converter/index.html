Japanese Era Date Converter

Turn a Gregorian date into its Japanese era year — Meiji, Taishō, Shōwa, Heisei or Reiwa — or an era date back into the civil calendar.

Direction

Era years count from the era's first year, so 2019 is both Heisei 31 and Reiwa 1. Dates from 1 January 1873 (Meiji 6) are supported, when Japan adopted the Gregorian calendar.

Japan has used one era name per reign since the Meiji era began in 1868: Meiji from 23 October 1868, Taishō from 30 July 1912, Shōwa from 25 December 1926, Heisei from 8 January 1989 and Reiwa from 1 May 2019. The first year of an era is also written gannen (元年), and it runs only to 31 December, so a civil year in which an emperor died or abdicated carries two era years: 1912 has both Meiji 45 and Taishō 1, 1989 has Shōwa 64 and Heisei 1, and 2019 has Heisei 31 and Reiwa 1. The era year is therefore the civil year minus the era's first year plus one, and an era date is checked against the day the era began or ended rather than the year alone. Japan adopted the Gregorian calendar with its months and days in 1873 (Meiji 6); earlier dates follow the lunisolar calendar and the older era names are not converted here.
