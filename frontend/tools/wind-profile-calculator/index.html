How the estimate is worked out. Both models assume the mean wind rises with height over flat, open ground in neutral weather. The power law multiplies the measured speed by the height ratio raised to the exponent you enter: the ratio is 1 for a neutral atmosphere over open land, 0.143 in decimal form, and about 0.11 over open water. The log law instead reads the profile from the terrain roughness length, multiplying the measured speed by the logarithm of each height over the roughness length; the terrain menu carries published roughness lengths from 0.0002 m for open sea to 2 m for a city centre, and it refuses heights below ten times the roughness length, where the model has no meaning. The wind-power ratio is the cube of the speed ratio, because the power in a moving air stream grows with the cube of its speed; it is not a turbine's output, which also depends on the machine's power curve, air density and losses. Both laws assume flat terrain with a long fetch upwind, so a ridge, a forest edge, a building or a valley can change the answer by more than the difference between the two models, and stable night air or gusty weather is not modelled. Everything is calculated in the browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
