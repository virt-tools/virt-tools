How the numbers are built

  • Frames per foot comes from the gauge. It is the number of frames that fills one foot of film, so it depends on how much film each frame advances: 12 inches divided by 0.75 in gives 16 frames per foot on 4-perf 35 mm, 21⅓ on 3-perf, 32 on 2-perf; the 7.62 mm frame pitch of 16 mm gives 40 frames per foot, Super 8 gives 72 and regular 8 mm gives 80.
  • Running time is frames divided by frame rate. The frame count is the footage multiplied by the frames per foot, rounded to the nearest whole frame, and divided by the frames per second of the camera.
  • Linear film speed follows from the same two numbers. Frames per second divided by frames per foot, times sixty, is how many feet of film pass the gate each minute: 90 ft/min for 4-perf 35 mm at 24 fps.
  • Reels are the footage over your load, rounded up. The final reel is reported with whatever footage it actually holds, so a load shorter than the footage counts as a second reel.

These are nominal gauge constants. Perforation pitch differs slightly between negative and print stock (short pitch and long pitch), and lab splices, leaders, camera overrun, edge-number spacing and the extra frames a camera loses when it starts and stops are not modelled, so a real camera report may run a frame or two either side of the figure. A length that holds less than one whole frame of the chosen gauge is refused rather than reported as zero. Everything runs locally in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
