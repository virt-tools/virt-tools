Enter the velocity factor and the measured round-trip delay to see the distance to the fault.

A reflectometer sends a pulse down the pair and times the echo, which travels out to the fault and back, so the cable distance is half the round-trip time multiplied by the signal speed. The signal speed is the velocity factor times the speed of light in vacuum, 299 792 458 m/s, and the dielectric constant of a homogeneous insulator is one over the velocity factor squared. Results are rounded to the millimetre and shown to two decimals. Velocity factors from 0.1 to 0.99, round-trip delays above zero and up to 1 000 000 ns, and lengths up to 100 000 m are accepted; text, exponent notation, a blank required field and out-of-range values are refused with the reason, and decimal commas work. This is arithmetic on the numbers you measured: it does not measure the cable, does not model dispersion, a splice, a splitter or a bridged tap, and cannot tell a fault from the far end of an unmatched cable. Verify a suspect run against a known-good cable and the meter’s own manual. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
