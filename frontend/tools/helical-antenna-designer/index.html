A helix radiates along its axis when its circumference is about one wavelength and the turns are spaced by roughly a quarter wavelength; that is the axial mode, and it is circularly polarised, so the winding hand sets the polarisation. The design here takes the winding you enter rather than guessing one: the wavelength is the speed of light over the frequency, the electrical circumference is the winding circumference over that wavelength, and the turn spacing is compared with it the same way. The gain estimate is fifteen times the square of the electrical circumference times the turns times the electrical spacing, in linear terms, reported in dBi; the half-power beamwidth is fifty-two degrees over the electrical circumference times the square root of the turns times the electrical spacing. The pitch angle is the arctangent of the spacing over the circumference, and the winding length is the turns times the spacing. These are the classical Kraus estimates: they assume a circular ground plane with a rim and an optimum pitch, and they read several decibels high against a flat ground plane, so treat the figure as a design target rather than a measurement. Wire diameter, feed matching, ground-plane size and the reflector's own gain are outside this calculator.
