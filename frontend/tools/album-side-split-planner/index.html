Add a tracklist to see the side split.

Both sides of a disc or tape share one total, so the split keeps each side under the medium's nominal limit while making the two sides as close in length as possible. A record is cut from the outside in, one continuous spiral per side, so the running order cannot be rearranged by the planner: only a single break in the list is chosen, which keeps every track on the side you sequenced it for. The planner tries every break point and keeps the one with the shorter longest side, breaking ties toward the smaller difference. Capacities are conservative nominal figures for full-level material: a 12 in LP at 33⅓ rpm about 22:00 per side, a 12 in single at 45 rpm about 12:00, a 10 in LP about 14:00, a 7 in single at 45 rpm about 4:30, a C-60 cassette 30:00 per side and a C-90 45:00 per side. Cut length on vinyl falls as level and bass rise, and tape capacity varies with tape stock and recorder, so keep a little headroom. Track gaps, fades, locked grooves, run-out and lead-in time are not counted, and the planner does not reorder tracks. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
