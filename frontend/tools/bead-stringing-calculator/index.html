    How the bead count is worked out

    • The clasp takes room first. The usable length is the finished length minus the clasp allowance, which covers the clasp and the end knots at both ends. Beads are fitted into what is left.
    • Each bead takes its diameter plus the spacer. The count is the largest whole number of beads whose diameter plus spacer fits the usable length, allowing for the fact that the last bead needs no spacer after it: beads = floor((usable + spacer) / (bead + spacer)). The beaded length is then beads × bead + (beads − 1) × spacer, and the remainder is the room left before the clasp.
    • Going the other way. With a bead count in hand, the beaded length is the same formula and the finished length adds the clasp allowance back on.
    • Cord. Cut the cord at the finished length plus a tail at each end for holding while knotting and for the clasp loops. The tool gives lengths and counts only: it does not judge bead hole size, cord thickness or the strength of a clasp, and everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
