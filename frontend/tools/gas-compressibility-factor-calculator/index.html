The pseudo-critical properties come from Sutton's 1985 correlation for hydrocarbon gases, Tpc = 169.2 + 349.5 γg − 74.0 γg² in °R and Ppc = 756.8 − 131.07 γg − 3.6 γg² in psia, with γg the gas gravity relative to air, so the reduced properties are Ppr = P / Ppc and Tpr = (T + 459.67) / Tpc. Z is the root of the Hall–Yarborough 1973 reduced-density equation, solved here by bisection over the reduced density Y between 0 and 1 and returned as Z = 0.06125 t Ppr e^(−1.2 (1−t)²) / Y with t = 1 / Tpr. Gas density is P M / (Z R T) with M = 28.97 γg lb/lbmol and R = 10.7316 psia·ft³/(lbmol·°R), reported in lb/ft³ and kg/m³; the formation volume factor is Bg = 0.02827 Z T / P ft³/scf, the reservoir cubic feet one standard cubic foot occupies at 14.7 psia and 60 °F. Gas gravity is accepted from 0.56 to 1.2, pressure from 100 to 10,000 psia and temperature from 60 to 300 °F, which keeps the reduced temperature above about 1.05. This is an estimate for a dry or lean hydrocarbon gas: the correlation is fitted to gases of a typical composition, no Wichert–Aziz or nitrogen correction is applied for H2S, CO2 or N2, and it is weakest near the critical point and for condensate wellstreams.
