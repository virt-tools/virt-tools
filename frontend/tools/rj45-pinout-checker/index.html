What each pair carries

  • Pins 1-2 and 3-6 (pairs 2 and 3) carry 10BASE-T and 100BASE-TX; 1000BASE-T and faster use all four pairs.
  • Power over Ethernet: Mode A feeds pairs 2 and 3, Mode B feeds pairs 1 and 4 (pins 4-5 and 7-8).
  • Pair grouping is the same in both standards — pins 1-2, 3-6, 4-5 and 7-8 — so a pair never straddles a different pair's pins; only which pair sits on pins 1-2 and 3-6 changes between T568A and T568B.

Pin numbers follow the 8P8C jack: with the plug's clip away from you and the gold contacts facing you, pin 1 is on the left. "RJ45" is the everyday name for the 8P8C modular plug. Modern Ethernet ports negotiate MDI/MDI-X on their own, so the usual lead uses the same standard at both ends; a crossover is only needed between two ports that cannot swap their transmit and receive pairs. This page covers the eight plug pins and nothing else: it does not model shielded or screened plugs, the colour code printed on a keystone jack's rear block, or the polarity a PoE injector applies. Nothing is stored and the page makes no network requests.
