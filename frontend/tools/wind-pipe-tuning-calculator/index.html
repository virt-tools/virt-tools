A tube open at both ends carries half a wavelength of its lowest note, and a tube stopped at one end a quarter, so the effective length is the speed of sound divided by twice the frequency (open) or four times it (stopped). Air outside each open end takes part in the vibration, so the page adds an end correction of 0.6133 times the bore radius at every open end and subtracts it to give the length to cut. The speed of sound follows 331.3 m/s × √(1 + θ/273.15) for the air temperature you enter. The figures assume a straight, smooth, thin-walled cylinder with an open end cut square; a real pipe's mouth, flue, tone holes, wall thickness, foot and bell all shift the pitch, so treat the length as a starting point and tune the pipe afterwards.
