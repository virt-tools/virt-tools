Each cue keeps its sequence number or identifier, its timestamp line and its position in the file; only the text lines are rebuilt. The tool joins the cue's words, finds the fewest lines that fit inside the character limit, and then spreads the words between those lines so the lines come out as even as possible — a 43-character cue at 42 characters per line becomes two balanced lines rather than one full line and one stray word. A line that starts with a dash starts a new run and keeps its own break, so two-speaker dialogue stays on separate lines; clear the checkbox to merge every line of a cue. Lengths count visible characters: markup such as <i> or <c.yellow> and entities such as &amp; are kept in the output but do not use up a character. A cue that needs more lines than the limit, a single word longer than the limit, and a cue whose text is over the reading-speed budget are listed but never rewritten or dropped — shortening the wording is an editorial decision. Reading speed is the cue's visible characters divided by its on-screen duration. Limits: 5 MB of text, 5,000 cues, 10–100 characters per line, 1–5 lines, 5–60 characters per second. Everything runs locally in this browser, with no uploads, storage or network requests.
