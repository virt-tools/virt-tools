How the class is worked out. Soil texture is the share of sand, silt and clay in the fine-earth fraction, measured by weight. The page turns the three settled depths into percentages and then applies the twelve class definitions in chapter 3 of the USDA Soil Survey Manual: sand is more than 85% sand with silt plus 1.5 times the clay under 15; loam is 7 to under 27% clay, 28 to under 50% silt and 52% or less sand; clay is 40% or more clay, 45% or less sand and under 40% silt, and each class between fills its own region of the triangle. Percentages that add up to within half a point of 100 are normalised to 100. A jar test measures settled volume, while the classes are defined on particle weight, and clay flocculates differently from sand, so a jar reading is an estimate of what a laboratory particle-size analysis would return; the deeper and more carefully you measure the bands, the closer it gets. Organic matter floating on top is not part of the mineral texture and is left out. Near a class boundary, repeat the test or send a sample to a lab. Everything is calculated in the browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
