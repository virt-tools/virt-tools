Wood Movement Calculator

Work out how much a board or panel shrinks or swells across its width when the air dries or damps, so a tabletop, drawer or frame-and-panel door can leave it room.

Measure the width across the grain, not the length. Room air settles kiln-dried stock near 6–9% in a heated house and 11–14% in a humid summer; a meter reading is better than a guess.

Wood moves as its moisture content follows the air, but only across the grain, and only below the fiber saturation point of about 30%, where the cell walls are saturated. The USDA Wood Handbook gives each species a total shrinkage from green to oven-dry, radially and tangentially, and treats the change as linear below that point: the shrinkage at moisture content x is Sx = S0 (1 − x / 30), with S0 taken from its Table 4–3. This page applies that relation to both readings, so a board measured at its current width is first scaled back to its green width and then forward to the new moisture content; the difference is the movement. Tangential values are used for flat-sawn widths and radial values for quarter-sawn, because wood moves about twice as much along the growth rings as across them. Average shrinkage varies by about 15% from board to board, reaction wood and juvenile wood move more, finish slows the change without stopping it, and a wide glue-up of mixed ring angles behaves like something between the two cases, so treat the result as an allowance rather than a measurement: leave it room in grooves, slots and breadboard ends instead of fastening a panel tight.
