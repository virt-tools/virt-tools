Drumhead Tuning Calculator

Relate a head's tension to the note it sounds: the fundamental and the first overtones of a drumhead, or the tension a target pitch needs.

The head's playing diameter across the hoop, not the shell's depth: 8 to 24 inches covers toms, snares and bass drums.

Film weight in grams per square metre: about 249 for 7 mil, 356 for 10 mil and 498 for 14 mil clear PET film.

The uniform tension in the head, in newtons per metre of circumference. A medium-tuned 14 inch head sits in the low thousands.

The pitch you want, in hertz: A4 is 440 Hz, A3 is 220 Hz and A2 is 110 Hz.

An ideal drumhead behaves like a circular membrane clamped at the hoop, and that model is what this page solves: the transverse wave speed is c = sqrt(T / sigma) for a uniform tension T in newtons per metre and an areal density sigma in kilograms per square metre, and the natural frequencies are f(m,n) = alpha(m,n) c / (2 pi a) for a head of radius a, where alpha(m,n) is the nth positive zero of the Bessel function J(m). The lowest mode, (0,1) at alpha = 2.404826, is the fundamental the drum sounds at; the next ones are (1,1) at 3.831706, (2,1) at 5.135622 and the second ring mode (0,2) at 5.520078, which is why a drum sings a chord of inharmonic overtones rather than a note with even harmonics. The same relation inverted gives the tension a target pitch needs, T = sigma (2 pi a f / alpha(0,1))^2. Real heads are not ideal membranes: the trapped air under the head adds mass, the film's bending stiffness and the collar and glue ring stiffen the edge, and the tension is never perfectly uniform, so a head sounds a few percent higher than this model predicts and the figure is an idealised membrane tension, not a lug torque or a DrumDial reading. The diameter is the playing diameter inside the hoop; a 14 inch head is 355.6 mm. Diameters are limited to 2 to 40 inches, areal density to 50 to 2,000 g/m², tension to 1 to 50,000 N/m and target pitch to 20 to 20,000 Hz, because beyond those the ideal membrane stops describing a drum; a pitch that would need a tension outside that band is refused rather than reported, since no head carries it. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network requests, and the page reports the model rather than a tuning recipe: tune by ear or by lug pitch and use this to see why a bigger head at the same tension sounds lower.
