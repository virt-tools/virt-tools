Cutting Board Glue-Up Planner

Work out how many strips to rip, how much lumber the glue-up eats with the saw kerf, and how many board feet an edge-grain or end-grain cutting board needs.

The strip width sets the board width in edge grain, and the board length in end grain. Kerf is the blade's cut width: about 3.2 mm for a full 1/8 in. ripping blade, 2.4 mm for a thin kerf. Stock thickness is what you buy; it must be at least the finished thickness.

An edge-grain board is a row of strips glued face to face with the wide face up: the strip width becomes part of the board width, and the stock thickness becomes the board thickness. An end-grain board is that same panel stood on edge: the strips are ripped as long as the panel, the panel is crosscut into slices as thick as the finished board, each slice is turned a quarter turn and glued face to face, so the strip width becomes part of the board length and the slice thickness becomes part of the board width. Every rip and crosscut removes a kerf, so the lumber consumed is the sum of the pieces plus one kerf between neighbouring strips or slices, and the trim allowance is cut off at the ends. Glue-line thickness, planing after the first glue-up, snipe and re-squaring the panel are not modelled, so buy a little extra stock; the board feet figure is the lumber as purchased, at the stock thickness entered. Everything is calculated in this page: nothing is stored and nothing is sent.
