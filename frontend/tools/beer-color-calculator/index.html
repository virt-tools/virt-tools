Beer Color Calculator

Turn a grain bill into an estimated beer color in SRM and EBC, with each malt's share of the color, from the weight, Lovibond rating and batch volume.

Use the volume the grain bill is brewed to — the batch size into the fermenter, not the kettle before the boil.

One row per malt in the recipe. The colour rating is on the malt spec sheet: pale base malt is around 1.5–2 °L, crystal malt 40–120 °L, roasted barley 300–600 °L.

The estimate follows Dan Morey's power fit to measured beer color. Each malt contributes malt color units, MCU = (pounds of grain × degrees Lovibond) ÷ gallons of batch volume, and the units add up over the bill: MCU = Σ(lb × °L) ÷ gal. Morey's curve turns that sum into a color, SRM = 1.4922 × MCU0.6859, which fits measured beers well below 50 SRM and flattens out above it, where 50 SRM is effectively black. EBC measures the same absorbance at 430 nm with a scale factor of 25 instead of 12.7, so EBC = SRM × 25 ÷ 12.7, about 1.97 × SRM; a color rating entered in °EBC is converted the same way before it enters the sum. Dark grains punch above their weight because weight and rating multiply before the curve is applied. Wort pH, boil time and vigor, kettle caramelization, fermentation and the beer's own haze all move the real measurement, and this predicts measured color rather than how dark the beer looks in a glass.
