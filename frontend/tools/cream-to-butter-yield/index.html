Cream to Butter Yield Calculator

Estimate the butter and buttermilk a batch of cream churns into.

Cream is close to 1 g per mL, so a US cup is about 235 g and a pint about 470 g.

Heavy cream is 36 percent fat. Butter must be at least 80 percent milkfat and is usually 80 to 86; buttermilk is normally under 1 percent fat. Salt is a share of the churned butter weight.

The cream is split into butter and buttermilk so that both the weight and the milkfat add up: butter plus buttermilk equals the cream, and the fat in each equals the fat in the cream. With the butter and buttermilk fat percentages you supply, that fixes the butter weight at cream weight times (cream fat minus buttermilk fat) over (butter fat minus buttermilk fat). Churn technique, washing, working and the water left in the butter all shift the real yield, and cream that has been homogenised or is very low in fat may not churn at all. The page only does the arithmetic: it makes no claim about food safety, shelf life or the standard of identity of the butter you make. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
