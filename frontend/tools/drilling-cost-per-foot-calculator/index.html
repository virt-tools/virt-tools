Drilling Cost per Foot Calculator

Turn a bit run into dollars per foot: the bit cost plus the rig time spent drilling and tripping, spread over the interval drilled, with the cost split into its three parts and the footage a target rate would need.

Enter the full operating cost of the rig per day, not only the drilling contractor's rate. The target is optional: leave it blank to skip the footage estimate.

One bit run, three costs: cost per foot = (bit cost + rig cost per hour × (time on bottom + round trip)) ÷ footage, with the rig rate taken as the daily rate ÷ 24. The rows split the same figure into the rig while drilling (rig rate ÷ rate of penetration), the round trip and the bit, which always add back to the total. With a target entered, the footage needed at the same rate of penetration is (bit + rig rate × round trip) ÷ (target − rig rate ÷ rate of penetration), which only exists above the drilling-only cost. The model assumes one bit, one fixed rig rate, no re-runs or repairs, no lost time and an interval measured from the same depth reference; it is a planning comparison of bit runs, not a well cost estimate, and it does not authorise drilling. Everything is computed in this browser: nothing is stored and nothing is sent.
