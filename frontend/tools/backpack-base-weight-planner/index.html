How the numbers work

  • Base weight is everything carried in the pack that is not worn and not used up: pack, shelter, sleep system, kitchen, clothing carried for later.
  • Total pack weight adds consumables — water, food, fuel — because you carry them at the start of a leg even though they get lighter.
  • Skin-out weight adds worn clothing and footwear, the full load your body moves.
  • Shares divide each figure by your body weight. A common rule of thumb is to keep total pack weight under about 20% of body weight; it is a starting point, not a limit that suits every trail, season or body.
  • Weights convert with 1 oz = 28.349523125 g and 1 lb = 453.59237 g. A single item may be at most 50 kg (50 000 g) and a pack may list up to 100 items; nothing is rounded until it is displayed.
  • This is arithmetic for planning a load, not medical or safety advice. Conditions, fitness, water availability and equipment ratings all matter. The page performs no storage, uploads or network requests.
