    Whole-pulse shutter angles

    PulsesShutter angleShutter speedExposurevs target

    Mains-powered tungsten, fluorescent and HMI lamps follow the supply voltage, so their output ripples at twice the mains frequency: 100 Hz on a 50 Hz supply and 120 Hz on a 60 Hz supply. Each frame integrates the light while the shutter is open, so an exposure that spans a whole number of ripples collects the same light every frame, while a fractional remainder leaves a brightness swing of roughly that fraction. For a ripple of f hertz the whole-pulse shutter speeds are k / f seconds, and at the frame rate p their angles are 360 × k × p / f degrees; the exposure is the angle divided by 360 and by the frame rate. The planner recommends the whole-pulse angle whose exposure is closest to your target in stops, breaking ties towards the shorter exposure. A ripple is treated as safe when the covered pulses are within 0.5% of a whole number. Custom frequencies cover dimmed LED fixtures whose own driver rate differs from the mains ripple. Rolling-shutter readout skew, aperture, ND filtration and camera-specific flicker filters are separate; this page assumes a uniform exposure over each frame. Everything is computed in your browser with no uploads, network access or storage.
