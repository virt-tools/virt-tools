Squeeze is how much the cross-section is flattened: the free cross-section minus the gland depth, in millimetres and as a share of the free cross-section. Stretch compares the groove bottom diameter with the free inside diameter, because a ring pulled over a larger diameter thins as it stretches, and the squeeze figure should be re-read on that thinner section when the stretch is more than a few percent. Gland fill divides the ring's cross-sectional area π CS²/4 by the groove's depth × width, and is the share of the groove the ring occupies cold: the remaining void has to absorb thermal expansion and any fluid swell. Parker's O-Ring Handbook gives a minimum squeeze of about 0.2 mm for any cross-section, a maximum of 30 % for a static seal and about 16 % for a dynamic one, asks for 60 % to 85 % gland fill (optimum near 75 %) so at least 10 % stays void, and keeps stretch around 5 % or less. This page checks those five figures only: it does not model the diametral clearance the ring may be extruded into, the surface finish, pressure, temperature, the compound or its swell, or the backup rings that a high-pressure gland needs, so it is a dimensional check rather than a seal design.
