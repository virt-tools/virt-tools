A sealed box trades volume for bass: a smaller box raises the system Q and the resonance, and rolls off sooner.

The driver's free-air resonance, from its data sheet.

Total Q at Fs, the driver's electrical and mechanical damping combined; most woofers sit between 0.2 and 0.5.

The volume of air with the same compliance as the driver's suspension, in litres.

0.5 is well damped, 0.577 is Bessel, 0.707 is maximally flat, 0.9 to 1.0 is punchier and peaks.

The net air volume behind the driver, after the driver, bracing and lining are taken out.

A sealed box adds an air spring behind the cone. That compliance sits in parallel with the suspension, so the system Q and the resonance rise by the same factor: Qtc = Qts × √(1 + Vas/Vb) and Fc = Fs × √(1 + Vas/Vb). Solved for the box, the first of those is Vb = Vas / ((Qtc/Qts)² − 1), which is why a target only a little above Qts asks for an enormous box, and why no sealed box can pull the system Q below the driver's own Qts. The response is a second-order high-pass, |H| = f² / √((f² − Fc²)² + (f · Fc / Qtc)²), so the level at Fc is 20 log₁₀(Qtc) dB, the −3 dB point solves the same expression for half power, and above Qtc = 0.707 the response peaks at Fc / √(1 − 1/(2 Qtc²)) by 20 log₁₀(Qtc / √(1 − 1/(4 Qtc²))) dB, a peak the page reports once it reaches 0.1 dB (around Qtc 0.77) because below that it is a ripple rather than a bump. Above the box the response is flat and below it the roll-off is 12 dB per octave, half the 24 dB of a ported box. Volume here is net: loose filling can add up to about 40 % to the effective volume, while bracing, the driver and any port take volume away. The model is the lumped Thiele/Small small-signal model: it takes the driver's parameters as constant with level and ignores box and driver losses, so a real cabinet usually lands a little flatter than these numbers, and it says nothing about cone excursion, power handling, a crossover, stuffing placement or the room.
