The pickup is treated as a voltage source in series with its winding inductance L and winding resistance Rdc, driving the cable capacitance C and the load R in parallel. That gives a second-order low-pass, H(s) = R / (s²LCR + s(L + R·C·Rdc) + R + Rdc), with an undamped resonance at f₀ = √((R + Rdc) / (LCR)) / 2π and a quality factor Q = √(LCR(R + Rdc)) / (L + R·C·Rdc). Above a Q of 0.71 the response peaks at f₀√(1 − 1/(2Q²)), and the peak stands Q / √(1 − 1/(4Q²)) above the flat level; below that it rolls off with no peak. The level at 2, 5 and 10 kHz is measured against the flat level, so 0 dB there means the pickup is unchanged at that frequency, and the corner is where the response has fallen 3 dB below the flat level. Only the cable capacitance is counted: the winding's own self-capacitance, any tone control, the pickup's position and the amplifier's input behaviour are left out, so a real guitar usually peaks a little lower and lower than shown. Cable capacitance also varies with construction, so measure it or take it from the cable's data sheet. Everything runs in the browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
