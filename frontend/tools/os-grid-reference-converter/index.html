Reading a National Grid reference

  • Two letters and two groups of figures. The letters name a 100 km square; the first group is the easting inside that square and the second the northing, both in metres. TG 51409 13177 is 51 409 m east and 13 177 m north of the south-west corner of square TG.
  • Fewer figures, coarser cell. Each figure dropped from a group multiplies the cell by ten: three figures per group is a 100 m square, two is 1 km. A reference names the south-west corner of its cell, so figures are truncated, never rounded up into the next cell.
  • Two steps, not one. The grid is drawn on the Airy 1830 ellipsoid in the OSGB36 datum, so a WGS84 position is first shifted by the seven-parameter Helmert transformation and then projected by Transverse Mercator with the central meridian at 2° W and a scale factor of 0.9996012717.

The datum shift here is the published approximate Helmert transformation, which Ordnance Survey rates at about 3.5 m (95%) in Great Britain; OSTN15, the OS Net geoid model, is the precise alternative when centimetres matter. The National Grid covers Great Britain only, so positions and squares outside it are refused rather than answered. Heights, Ordnance Datum Newlyn and offshore areas are not modelled. Nothing is stored and the page makes no network requests.
