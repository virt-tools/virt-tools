Court and paint

ITF Rule 1 allows 2.5 to 5 cm for the court lines, up to 10 cm for the baselines; the centre service line and centre mark are fixed at 5 cm. The paint figure here uses one width for every line.

Take the spreading rate from the paint tin, usually 6 to 12 m² per litre for a court line paint. The result is one coat.

The ITF Rules of Tennis fix the court at 23.77 m from baseline to baseline, 8.23 m wide for singles and 10.97 m for doubles, with the service lines 6.40 m from the net, drawn only between the singles sidelines, so the 1.37 m doubles alleys never widen a service box. The centre service line divides each service area into two boxes 6.40 m deep and 4.11 m wide, and every baseline carries a 10 cm centre mark. All measurements are to the outside of the lines, which is why the tape checks in the table use the published figures rather than recomputed halves. The net runs 12.80 m between post centres for doubles, its posts 0.914 m outside the doubles court, and stands 1.07 m at the posts and 0.914 m at the centre; for singles on a combined court the net is held by singles sticks 0.914 m outside each singles sideline, and a singles-only court uses a 10 m net with its posts in the same place. The run-back figures are the ITF's recommended minimum distances between the baselines and the backstops and between the sidelines and the sidestops, 5.48 m and 3.05 m for club and recreational play, 6.40 m and 3.66 m for international competition; local planning rules and the space for umpires, benches and fencing may ask for more. The painted area is the summed line length times the line width, without deducting the small overlaps where lines cross, and the paint volume is that area divided by the coverage you enter, so buy a little more than one coat needs. This is marking arithmetic: it does not measure your ground, and a court still has to be squared from the net line with the tapes. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
