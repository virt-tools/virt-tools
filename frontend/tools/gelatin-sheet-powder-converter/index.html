    How the conversion works

    • Bloom measures strength per gram. A gelatin's Bloom number is the force in grams needed to push a standard probe 4 mm into a 6.67 % gel held at 10 °C. Higher Bloom means more setting power from the same weight.
    • Bloom-grams are the common currency. Multiplying mass by Bloom number gives a rough estimate of total setting power, so 2 g of 200 Bloom and 1.6 g of 250 Bloom both come to 400 bloom-grams.
    • Sheet weights differ by grade. Bronze and silver leaf are commonly 2.5 g, gold 2 g and platinum 1.7 g, with typical strengths of about 140, 160, 200 and 250 Bloom. Makers vary, so weigh a sheet or read the packet when it gives its own numbers.
    • The estimate is linear. Real gels do not scale perfectly with mass times Bloom, and soaking, blooming and melting losses are ignored. Expect a few percent difference, not an exact match.
    • This converts quantities only. It does not set a recipe's gel strength, and it is not food-safety advice. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network requests.
