A bass reflex port is a Helmholtz resonator: the air in the tube is the mass and the air in the box is the spring, so the tuning is f = (c / 2π) × √(A / (V × L_eff)), with A the total port area, V the net box volume and L_eff the tube length plus its end correction. Both ends of a tube open into free air, and the standard engineering correction is L_eff = L + 0.3 × D, where D is the inside diameter, so a short fat port is tuned lower than its bare length suggests. The speed of sound is taken as 343 m/s, the value in dry air at 20 °C; a cold room tunes the box slightly flat and a hot one slightly sharp. Enter a length to read the tuning, or a tuning to read the length you have to cut. The page models the port alone: it does not know the woofer's Thiele/Small parameters, so it cannot tell you the box volume the driver wants, the −3 dB point, or how the port and the driver sum, and it does not model flared ends, slot ports, passive radiators or the port's own air speed. Two practical consequences are worth stating: the tube and the driver both take up air volume that has to come out of the gross box, and a port whose area is small for its box moves air fast and chuffs at high levels, so the page reports the area per litre as information rather than as a verdict. The fit check compares the cut length with the cube root of the box volume, a rough stand-in for the internal depth, so a tube longer than that needs an elbow, a slot port or an external run.
