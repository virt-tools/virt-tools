Daylight Saving Transition Finder

Find every clock change in a time zone for a chosen year — the local date, the hour that skips or repeats, and the UTC offset on each side. Useful for planning meetings, travel connections and schedules that cross a changeover. Rules come from the IANA time-zone database that ships with your browser, so everything is computed locally.

A daylight-saving transition is the moment a zone switches between its standard and summer offsets. The change instant is stored in UTC; the local clock on the earlier side of it is the time shown here, so a spring-forward reads 02:00 → 03:00 and an autumn change reads 02:00 → 01:00. Not every zone changes: most of Asia, Africa and the tropics keep one offset all year, and a few regions shift by 30 minutes instead of an hour. Offsets and dates follow the IANA time-zone database embedded in your browser, which reflects legislation in force when that copy was built — a government can change or abolish a transition after your browser shipped, and historical years before 1970 depend on the database's back-zone data. Times are wall-clock times in the chosen zone, so a zone that shifts its whole offset by law shows here as a clock change too. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
