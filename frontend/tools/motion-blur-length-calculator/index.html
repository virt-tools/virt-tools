The shutter is open for the shutter angle divided by 360 and by the frame rate, so a 180° shutter at 24 fps exposes for 1/48 s. During that time the subject travels its speed times the exposure, and the lens projects that travel onto the sensor at the focal length divided by the subject distance: travel times that magnification is the smear on the sensor, and the sensor width divided by the capture width turns it into pixels. The result is the blur of a subject moving across the frame at a constant speed on one plane — a camera pan of one radian per second smears the same as a subject moving at that many metres per second at that distance, and anything nearer or farther shifts by a different amount. Depth of field, focus breathing, rolling shutter, stabilisation and lens distortion are not modelled, so check a test frame before committing to the look.
