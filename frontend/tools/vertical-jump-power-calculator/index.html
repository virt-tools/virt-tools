Jump height is the difference between standing reach and reach at the top of the jump, or you can derive it from a measured flight time with h = g·t²/8, using g = 9.80665 m/s². Takeoff velocity follows from v = √(2gh) and the flight time from t = 2√(2h/g), so height, time and velocity are three views of the same jump. Peak power comes from two published regressions on squat-jump height and body mass: Sayers et al. (1999), 60.7 × height (cm) + 45.3 × mass (kg) − 2055, and Harman et al. (1991), 61.9 × height (cm) + 36.0 × mass (kg) − 1822. Both were fitted to jump-and-reach scores against force-plate measurements, so they estimate mechanical power at takeoff to within a few hundred watts rather than measuring the force you produce. A mat, a phone app and a wall test each measure height with different errors, and arm swing, landing and technique all move the result. The tool refuses a height-and-mass pair at which either regression returns a non-positive number. Metric input is centimetres and kilograms, imperial is inches and pounds, and flight time is always in seconds; blank, non-numeric, exponent-notation and out-of-range values are refused with the failing field named. Nothing is stored and the page makes no network requests.
