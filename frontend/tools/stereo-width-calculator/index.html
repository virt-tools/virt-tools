Narrow or widen a stereo pair and see where its peaks land. Give the left and right channel levels in dBFS and a width percentage: below 100 % both channels move toward the mono mid signal, above 100 % they move apart.

0 dBFS is full scale. 0 % is mono, 100 % leaves the pair untouched, 200 % doubles the side gain.

A stereo pair splits into a mid signal M = (L + R) / 2, the part that survives a mono fold-down, and a side signal S = (L - R) / 2, the part that cancels. A width w rebuilds the channels as L' = M + wS and R' = M - wS and applies only to the difference between them, which is why equal channels come back unchanged at any width. Levels are peak amplitudes converted from dBFS (10^(dB/20)), and both channels are assumed to peak at the same instant, the worst case for a width change; decorrelated program material peaks lower. Because L' + R' = 2M, the mono fold-down is exactly the mid signal and does not move with width. Extreme widening can leave one output negative, which is a polarity inversion rather than a level. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
