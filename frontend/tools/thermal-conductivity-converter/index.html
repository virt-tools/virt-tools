Thermal Conductivity Converter

Convert a thermal conductivity between SI and imperial units — watts per metre-kelvin, Btu per hour-foot-degree Fahrenheit, Btu-inch per hour-square foot-degree Fahrenheit, and the calorie-based units that older handbooks still use. A reference table of typical material values sits below the result.

MaterialW/(m·K)Btu·in/(h·ft²·°F)

One Btu/(h·ft·°F) is about 1.7307 W/(m·K) and one Btu·in/(h·ft²·°F) is about 0.1442 W/(m·K); the Btu-inch form carries the thickness in inches, so it is a conductivity rather than a conductance. These are material properties, not U-values or R-values: divide by thickness to get conductance. Reference values are typical handbook figures for pure metals, common alloys and dry insulation, and real samples move with alloy, density, moisture and temperature. Results are rounded to six significant digits. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
