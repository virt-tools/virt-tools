Moving the sight pin by d changes the angle between the line of sight and the arrow by d ÷ sight radius, so at a distance D the point of impact moves by D × d ÷ sight radius. Inverted, the correction is d = sight radius × error ÷ D, with the error and the distance in the same unit. Chase the arrow: move the sight toward the group, so a group that is high needs the sight raised and the group then lands lower; a group to the right needs the sight moved right. Measured clicks divide that movement by the sight's click value. Typical recurve sight radii are 600 to 900 mm, and compound sights are often shorter. This is a geometric estimate: it assumes the sight radius is measured along the line of sight to the pin, and it ignores arrow speed, trajectory shape and form errors, so verify a large change with a test group.
