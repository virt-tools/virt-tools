Branch-Line Coupler Designer

Design a single-section 3 dB branch-line hybrid in a Z0 system: the arm impedances, the guided wavelength and arm lengths, and what the coupler does away from the design frequency.

Leave the analysis frequency empty to see the design point. The two series arms come out at Z0/√2 and the two shunt arms at Z0; the physical lengths follow the velocity factor.

Each arm is a quarter wave at the design frequency. The two series arms that carry the signal across the top and bottom of the ring are Z0/√2 and the two shunt arms are Z0, so a 50 Ω system wants 35.355 Ω and 50 Ω lines. The lengths are a quarter of the guided wavelength λg = c · vf / f. Away from f0 the response is computed from the admittance matrix of the four ideal lines, S = (I − Z0Y)(I + Z0Y)−1, with every port terminated in Z0; the 20 dB bandwidth is the span over which both the return loss and the isolation stay at or above 20 dB. The model is ideal and lossless: it ignores conductor and dielectric loss, the T-junction discontinuity that makes a real coupler slightly shorter than a quarter wave, dispersion and fabrication tolerance. At the design frequency an ideal hybrid is perfectly matched, so return loss and isolation are shown as 60 dB or better. Everything is computed in this browser: nothing is stored and nothing is sent.
