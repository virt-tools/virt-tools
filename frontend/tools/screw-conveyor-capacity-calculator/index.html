A screw conveyor is a volumetric machine: one turn moves the material that fills the annular flight area over one pitch. The CEMA form used here is Q = 0.7854 · (Ds² − Dp²) · P · K · 60 / 1728 cubic feet per hour per revolution, with the diameters and the pitch in inches and K the trough loading as a fraction; in metric terms the same figure is π/4 · (D² − d²) · P · n · 60 · K with D, d, P in metres and n in revolutions per minute. Multiplying by the loose bulk density gives the mass rate. The default 300 mm screw on a 76 mm pipe, standard 300 mm pitch, 60 rpm and 30% loading carries 21.4 m³/h, about 17.1 t/h of an 800 kg/m³ powder. This is a level-trough estimate for a standard full flight: an inclined conveyor loses capacity (roughly 10% at 5°, 20% at 10°, 30% at 15° and 35% at 20°), cut, ribbon or paddle flights carry less, a screw feeder flood-loaded under a hopper runs far fuller than a control-fed conveyor, and lump size, abrasiveness, hanger bearings, the drive and the material's tendency to arch are not checked here. Loading above 45% is outside the trough-loading range the selection tables are built on and is refused rather than reported. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
