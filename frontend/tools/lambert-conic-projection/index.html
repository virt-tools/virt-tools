Lambert Conformal Conic Projection Calculator

Convert between latitude and longitude and the easting and northing of a conic map defined by two standard parallels, and read the scale factor and grid convergence at that point.

Map definition

Degrees, negative for south and west. The cone touches the ellipsoid along the two standard parallels, where scale is exactly 1; give the same value twice for a single-parallel cone. The origin sits where the latitude of origin crosses the central meridian, and the false easting and northing shift the grid away from it.

Point

Decimal degrees, negative for south and west. Decimal commas are accepted.

A Lambert conformal conic map wraps the ellipsoid in a cone that cuts it along two standard parallels. With the semi-major axis a, eccentricity e and the cone constant n, the map uses m(φ) = cos φ / sqrt(1 − e² sin²φ), t(φ) = tan(π/4 − φ/2) / ((1 − e sin φ)/(1 + e sin φ))^(e/2), n = ln(m₁/m₂) / ln(t₁/t₂) for two parallels or n = sin φ₁ for one, F = m₁ / (n t₁ⁿ) and ρ = a F tⁿ. The point lands at x = ρ sin(n Δλ) and y = ρ₀ − ρ cos(n Δλ) from the origin, where Δλ is the longitude measured from the central meridian and ρ₀ is ρ at the latitude of origin, then the false easting and northing are added. The scale factor k = n ρ / (a m) is exactly 1 on each standard parallel, slightly below 1 between them and grows outside them; grid convergence is n Δλ, the angle between grid north and true north, positive east of the central meridian. The map is conformal, so angles and small shapes are preserved everywhere even though scale varies. Latitudes are limited to the range between the poles, the standard parallels must lie on one side of the equator and neither they nor the latitude of origin may sit on a pole, and no datum shift is applied: the ellipsoid you choose is the one the map is defined on. Everything runs in your browser with no upload, storage or network access, and the page does not draw the map.
