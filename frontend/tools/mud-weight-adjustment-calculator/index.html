Mud Weight Adjustment Calculator

Raise a drilling mud weight with barite or cut it by dilution: the weighting material in pounds and 100 lb sacks, or the light fluid in barrels, with the volume change and the final specific gravity.

Barite is taken at 4.2 SG, or 35.0 ppg. The dilution fluid is only used when the target is below the current weight; leave it at 8.33 ppg for fresh water, or enter the base-oil density for an oil-based mud.

Both answers are one mass balance. Weighting up: pounds of barite = 1,470 × V × (W2 − W1) ÷ (35 − W2) for a volume V in barrels, which is the same number as 100 lb sacks per 100 bbl; each sack adds 0.068 bbl, so the volume grows by the barite volume. Cutting weight: barrels of light fluid = V × (W1 − W2) ÷ (W2 − Dw), where Dw is the dilution fluid density, 8.33 ppg for fresh water. The model assumes dry weighting material, no losses to the shakers, volumes measured at one temperature and a fully mixed system, and it does not check the formation pressure, the equivalent circulating density or the mud programme: those are set by the drilling engineer. Everything is computed in this browser: nothing is stored and nothing is sent.
