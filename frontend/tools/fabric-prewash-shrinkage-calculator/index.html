Enter the finished size and the shrinkage to see the cut size.

Fabrics are cut oversize because the first wash takes up length and width. The cut size is the finished size divided by one minus the shrinkage fraction, so a 5% shrink on 100 cm needs 105.26 cm cut. Length runs along the selvedge (warp) and width across it (weft); the two directions usually shrink by different amounts, and a wash test is the reliable way to measure them — mark a square on a scrap, wash and dry it exactly as the finished piece will be, and re-measure. Loose weaves, viscose and linen can take up far more than the same fabric in a tight weave. The length on hand is checked against the cut length only; width and any pattern repeat are not modelled, and a printed repeat may need extra fabric to match.
