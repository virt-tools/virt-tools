Heat Recovery Effectiveness Checker

Measure four duct temperatures at an HRV or ERV and see how much of the heat the core actually recovers.

Station 1: the fresh-air duct before the core.
Station 3: the stale-air duct from the house.
Station 2: the fresh-air duct into the house.
Station 4: the stale-air duct to outside, used for the balance check.
Changing the unit converts the four readings.
From the unit's spec sheet, for the rating allowance.
Rated or measured airflow, for the recovered load.

Take the readings with the unit at its normal speed, after ten minutes at steady conditions, and not while a defrost cycle is running.

Effectiveness is the supply-air temperature change from outdoor air divided by the difference between return and outdoor air. A passive core cannot push the supply air past the return air or back past the outdoor air.

This page follows the sensible-effectiveness equation of AHRI Standard 1060 (I-P) 2018, Appendix C, Equation C1: effectiveness is the supply-air temperature change from the outdoor air, divided by the difference between the return air and the outdoor air, scaled by the ratio of the minimum capacity rate to the supply capacity rate. At balanced supply and exhaust airflow the two capacity rates are equal and the ratio is one, which leaves (supply − outdoor) ÷ (return − outdoor), the arithmetic here; with unbalanced airflow the mass-flow ratio has to be applied and this page does not do that. Only dry-bulb temperature is used, so the result is sensible effectiveness: an ERV's latent and total effectiveness need humidity ratios and are not modelled. The air leaving the exhaust side is used only as a balance check, because with balanced airflow the supply should gain the heat that the exhaust loses. The comparison band borrows the published-rating allowance of AHRI 1060 section 5.1.1, where a measured value may sit up to four relative percentage points plus one absolute point below the rating, or two points, whichever is larger, but a field check with hand-held probes is not a conforming ASHRAE 84 rating test: a reading under the band is a reason to repeat the measurement, not proof that the core is faulty. The recovered load is 1.08 × CFM × temperature change, where 1.08 is 60 minutes × 0.075 lb/ft³ × 0.24 Btu/lb·°F for standard air at sea level. In cooling the same equation gives a negative supply-air change and the page reports the size of it. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
