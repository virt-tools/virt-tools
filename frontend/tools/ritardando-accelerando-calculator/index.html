Tempo is quarter-note beats per minute, so one beat lasts 60 ÷ BPM seconds. With the curve linear in time, the tempo rises or falls by the same amount every second; the beats covered grow quadratically, and the whole change lasts 120 × beats ÷ (start + end) seconds. With the curve linear in beats, the tempo rises or falls by the same amount on every beat, so each beat lasts 60 ÷ its own tempo, and the change lasts 60 × beats × ln(end ÷ start) ÷ (end − start) seconds — the logarithmic mean of the two tempos. Equal start and end tempos give beats × 60 ÷ start on either curve, and the beat-linear ramp always runs slightly longer than the time-linear one. Everything is calculated in this page: nothing is uploaded, stored or fetched. Published figures are rounded to three decimals while the page formats them for display.
