Enter an availability target to see how much downtime it allows.

The budget is the share of the period the target leaves for outages: with target p percent over a period of P hours, the allowed downtime is (1 − p/100) × P × 3600 seconds, so 99.9 % of a 730 h month allows 43 min 48 s. If you enter the downtime you saw, the achieved availability is (1 − downtime ÷ period) × 100 percent and the share of the budget used is the downtime divided by the allowed downtime. Seconds are rounded to 0.01, the achieved availability to 0.0001 percent and the share of the budget to 0.1 percent. Targets above 0 and up to 99.9999 % are accepted, periods are the fixed lengths in the list, and downtime may be anything from 0 up to the length of the period; text, exponent notation and out-of-range values are refused with the reason, and decimal commas work. A “nine” is one more 9 in the target, so 99.99 % is four nines. This is arithmetic on the target and the downtime you supply: it does not measure availability, does not know how your monitoring counts a partial failure, and excludes nothing — planned maintenance usually counts unless your SLA says otherwise. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
