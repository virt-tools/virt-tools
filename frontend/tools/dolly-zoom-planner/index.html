The subject keeps the same size because its magnification is the focal length divided by the subject distance minus the focal length, and that ratio is held constant: the end focal length is the start focal length times the end distance over the start distance. Each camera position in the table follows the same rule, so a move from 3 m to 1.5 m halves the focal length and keeps the framing. The background sits at a finite distance, so its size relative to the subject changes by the ratio of the two background magnifications — the dolly-in/zoom-out form pushes the background away, and the dolly-out/zoom-in form pulls it closer. Distances are measured from the lens rather than the camera body; focus, depth of field, lens zoom curves, focus breathing, rolling-shutter changes and the framing of the subject's own edges are not modelled, and the subject is treated as flat at the stated distance.
