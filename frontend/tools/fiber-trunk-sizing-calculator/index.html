Fiber Trunk Sizing Calculator

Turn the duplex circuits you have to serve into fiber strands, multifiber trunk cables and the connectors at each end. The planner adds your spare allowance, rounds up to whole MPO trunks and shows how many strands that rounding leaves unused.

Enter whole duplex circuits and a spare allowance from 0 to 100 percent with at most one decimal place. The spare is added to the circuits before they are doubled into strands, so a 25 percent allowance on 48 circuits plans 60 pairs and 120 strands.

A duplex circuit needs two strands, one in each direction. The pairs to provision are the circuits plus the spare allowance, rounded up to a whole pair, and the strands required are twice that. Because a trunk cable carries a fixed number of fibers, the strands you buy are the smallest whole number of trunks that covers the requirement, so the installed count is usually a little above the plan. The difference is reported as unused strands, and the strand utilisation is the required count divided by the installed count. Each trunk terminates in one MPO connector at each end and each duplex pair is presented on one LC duplex adapter at each end, so the adapters equal the installed strand count and the LC connectors, one per strand end, are twice that. Every strand also gets an LC connector at each end. The planner compares 8-, 12- and 24-fiber trunks for the same requirement and names a smaller-waste option when one exists. Duplex circuit counts, spare percentages and these connector conventions are planning arithmetic, not an installation standard; confirm the trunk types, polarity method, cassette mapping and spare policy with your cabling designer and the parts you actually order.
