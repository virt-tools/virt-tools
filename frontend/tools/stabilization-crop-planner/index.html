A stabiliser can only move the picture inside the recorded frame, so every pixel of shake or roll has to be paid for with a margin the crop can slide into. The margin is the residual shake plus the roll in radians multiplied by half the frame diagonal, the farthest a corner travels when the frame turns about its centre, rounded up to a whole pixel. Keeping the shot's shape means zooming uniformly, so the short side sets the zoom: the crop is scaled up until the short side has that margin on both edges, the long side then gives up the same share of its pixels, and both sides land on even pixel counts. The crop budget table inverts the relation: for a chosen zoom it gives the short-side margin and the largest shake that still fits beside the roll. Shake and roll are what you measure or expect after stabilisation, so this plans a crop rather than predicting what a particular stabiliser will do; off-centre crops, rolling-shutter skew, parallax between near and far subjects and resampling softening are not modelled. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
