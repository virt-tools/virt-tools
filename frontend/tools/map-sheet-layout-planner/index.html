A sheet printed at 1:25,000 turns each millimetre of paper into 25 metres of ground, so the printable width and height of the page, divided by the margin, become the ground coverage of one sheet. The page then lays those sheets in a rectangular grid, with the stated overlap repeated at every join, and rounds the count up: a map that runs even one millimetre past the last sheet needs another one. The figures assume a single uniform scale across the whole map, which is how a printed topographic map is drawn but not how a small-scale projection behaves over a continent; they also ignore the distortion of the projection, the width of the printer's unprintable edge beyond the margin you enter, and any trimming. Nothing is sent or stored: the page runs entirely in your browser.
