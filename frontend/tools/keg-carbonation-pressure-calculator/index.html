Keg Carbonation Pressure Calculator

What pressure to set on the CO2 regulator: enter the beer temperature, the carbonation you want in volumes of CO2 and the altitude, and read the equilibrium gauge pressure.

The chart covers 33–42 °F (0.6–5.6 °C) and 2.1–3.1 volumes; outside that range the row line is extended to 2.0–3.6 volumes.

This is the equilibrium gauge pressure for pure CO2 — the pressure at which the beer neither gains nor loses carbonation. It comes from the Brewers Association’s draught carbonation chart (ASBC 1949 data, taken for beer at specific gravity 1.015 and 4.8 % ABV): each printed row is a straight line, because Henry’s law makes dissolved gas proportional to absolute pressure. The page fits every row to its eleven printed points, interpolates between the 1 °F rows, and adds the published 1 psi per 2,000 ft of altitude. Beer-line balancing, blended gas and bottle priming sugar are separate jobs. Everything runs in your browser.
