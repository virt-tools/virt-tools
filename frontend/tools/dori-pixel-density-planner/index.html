DORI is the pixel density a scene needs for four surveillance tasks: detection at 25 px/m, observation at 62.5 px/m, recognition at 125 px/m and identification at 250 px/m of the subject, the last being about 40 pixels across a 0.16 m human face. Because a rectilinear lens spreads the same number of pixels across a scene that widens with distance, density is horizontal pixels divided by the scene width 2 × distance × tan(field of view / 2), so it falls with the square of nothing but the distance — doubling the distance halves the density, and the reach of each level is the distance at which that division lands exactly on the level's density. Square pixels mean the vertical density matches the horizontal one. The figures assume a level camera looking square at the face, even lighting and a sharp, undistorted image; they are a planning guide rather than a guarantee, because motion blur, low light, compression and a face turned away all cost pixels in practice.
