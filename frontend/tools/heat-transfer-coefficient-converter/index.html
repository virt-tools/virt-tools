One Btu/(h·ft²·°F) is about 5.6783 W/(m²·K). A heat transfer coefficient is a conductance: multiply it by an area and a temperature difference to get a heat flow, in watts or in Btu per hour. Its reciprocal is a resistance — the imperial R-value in h·ft²·°F/Btu — and imperial R-values of the layers in a wall add in series before you invert them again. The table holds round reference points for checking a conversion, not recommendations for any particular assembly. Results are rounded to six significant digits.
