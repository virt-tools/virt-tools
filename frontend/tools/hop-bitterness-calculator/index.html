Hop Bitterness Calculator

Add up the bitterness a hop bill contributes, in IBUs, from the weight, alpha acid percentage and boil time of each addition in your kettle volume and boil gravity.

Use the volume and gravity the hops actually boil in — the kettle figures, not the topped-up fermenter ones.

Each addition is weighed and boiled on its own; the model adds them up. Alpha acid percentages are printed on the hop package.

The estimate uses the Tinseth utilisation model, the most widely used way to predict bitterness in home brewing. Utilisation is split into a gravity factor, 1.65 × 0.000125(SG − 1), which falls as the wort gets denser, and a boil-time factor, (1 − e−0.04t) ÷ 4.15, which rises with time and flattens out. Their product is the share of the alpha acids that isomerise; the bittering contribution is that share times the milligrams of alpha acid per litre of kettle volume, and IBU is exactly one milligram of isomerised alpha acid per litre. Because the model only covers the boil, a flame-out or whirlpool addition entered as 0 minutes contributes nothing here, and hop stands and dry hopping are not counted — treat the figure as a floor for those beers. Whole-cone hops are the basis; pellets usually isomerise a little better, and kettle geometry, boil vigour and pH all move the real number. Measured IBU also does not predict perceived bitterness, which depends on malt sweetness, roast and carbonation. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
