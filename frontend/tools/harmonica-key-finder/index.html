Positions step down the circle of fourths inside the harp's own scale: 1st position plays in the harp's key, 2nd (cross harp) a fourth below it, 3rd another fourth below, and so on to the 7th. A major-key song usually takes 1st or 2nd position; a minor or bluesy song often takes 3rd or 4th.

Blow and draw layout

Top note is a blow, bottom note is a draw; • marks the root of the key being played.

A 10-hole Richter diatonic is built on one major scale: the blow notes are the root, third and fifth and the draw notes the second, fifth, seventh, second, fourth and sixth, repeated up the harp. Each position plays a different mode of that same scale, so the harp that plays a song in A in 2nd position is the D harp, whose notes are A mixolydian. Notes are spelled in the harp's own key, so a song in D flat can read as C sharp on an F sharp harp. The natural layout holds every note of every position; notes outside the low octave need bends or overblows in practice. This models the common 10-hole diatonic only, not tremolo, chromatic or alternate tunings, and it stores, uploads and transmits nothing.
