    What this does not do

    • This is planning arithmetic, not nutrition or medical advice. It does not adjust for children, pregnancy, illness, allergies, special diets or pets, and it does not check that the food you store is something your household will actually eat.
    • The calories per person per day figure is yours to set. The 2,000 default is the value the FDA uses as a general guide for nutrition advice; it is not a personal requirement, and real intake varies with age, size and activity.
    • It assumes the food you count is edible, in date and stored as its label requires. It does not track expiry dates, storage temperature, spoilage or rotation, and it does not deduct the food you would eat anyway.
    • Package sizes are generic meal counts, not product specifications. Check the label of whatever you buy: a case of 24 meals may hold 24 small meals or 12 large ones.
    • It plans food only. Water, cooking fuel, a heat source, a can opener and any special infant or medical food are not included, and reloading the page clears your entries.

    Foods to consider

    From Ready.gov's Food page: store at least a several-day supply of non-perishable food, and choose foods your household will eat.

    • Protein or fruit bars
    • Dry cereal or granola
    • Peanut butter
    • Dried fruit
    • Canned juices
    • Non-perishable pasteurized milk
    • High-energy foods
    • Food for infants
    • Comfort and stress foods
    • A manual can opener for anything canned
