Three-Wire Thread Measurement

Work out the three-wire check for a 60° thread: the best wire size, the micrometer reading the wires should give over a nominal thread, and the pitch diameter behind a reading you already took.

Thread

Metric: M6x1, M10×1.5. Unified: 1/4-20 UNC, #10-24, 0.375-16. 60° threads only — Acme, Whitworth and multi-start threads need other constants.

Your measurement (optional)

In millimetres for a metric thread, inches for a unified one; a matching unit such as mm may follow the number. Leave both blank to get the reading the best wire should give.

Reading over the wires

Three wires of equal diameter sit in the thread grooves — two on one side, one opposite — and a micrometer reads across them. For a 60° thread the wire touches the flanks, so the basic pitch diameter is E = D − 0.649519P, the best wire that touches it at the pitch line is W = 0.57735P, and the reading over the wires is M = E + 3W − 0.866025P. Solving that for a reading you took gives E = M + 0.866025P − 3W, which is the same as subtracting the constant 3W − 0.866025P printed on thread-wire charts. Every error in the wire is multiplied by three, so the wires must be clean, straight and the size stated; the formula ignores lead angle and flank-angle error, which is why it is for single-start threads with a low lead angle. A wire at or above 0.866P bottoms out in the groove instead of seating on the flanks. Whether the pitch diameter passes belongs to the thread's own limits (ISO 965 for metric, ASME B1.1 for unified), which this page does not apply.
