meminfo Decoder

Paste the contents of /proc/meminfo and read the breakdown free reports: how much RAM is in use, what is genuinely available, how much sits in buffers and page cache, and how much of swap is spent.

Paste the whole file from cat /proc/meminfo, or just the lines you have. Values are read in kB, blank lines and # comments are ignored, and a repeated field is refused.

Each line is read as a field name, a colon, a whole number and an optional kB unit; blank lines and # comments are skipped, a field repeated twice is refused, and a value in any other unit is refused. MemTotal and MemFree are required. Total RAM is MemTotal. In use is MemTotal minus MemAvailable, the figure free(1) documents for its used column; a dump without MemAvailable falls back to MemFree plus Buffers and Cached as the available figure, and to MemTotal minus Free, Buffers and Cached as in use, which is what kernels before 3.14 allowed. Buff/cache is Buffers + Cached + SReclaimable, matching the cache and buff/cache columns of free(1), and shared memory is Shmem. Kernel stacks and page tables add KernelStack, PageTables and SecPageTables. Swap in use is SwapTotal minus SwapFree. Every share is that field divided by MemTotal, or by SwapTotal for the swap rows, rounded half up to one decimal. The two bars partition RAM into in use and available and swap into in use and free, so each bar adds to exactly its total. Sizes are shown in KiB, MiB or GiB by magnitude. The page reads only the text you paste: it does not query the host, so cgroup limits, watermarks and reclaim behaviour are not modelled, and nothing here is a diagnosis of the machine. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent, stored or uploaded.
