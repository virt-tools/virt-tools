How the gain is worked out

Sensitivity is quoted at 1 kHz with 1 pascal of sound pressure, and 1 Pa is 94 dB SPL. A figure in mV/Pa is the output voltage at that reference; dBV/Pa is the same thing as 20 log10(V / 1 V), so mV/Pa = 1000 × 10^(dBV/20) and 10 mV/Pa is −40 dBV/Pa. Levels in dBu are referenced to 0.775 V, so the mic output is 20 log10(S / 0.775) + (SPL − 94) dBu, with S in volts per pascal.

  • The preamp adds its gain in decibels on top of that figure: 20 dB of gain is ten times the voltage.
  • The interface clips when the signal reaches the level quoted as its maximum input, which is 0 dBFS. A converter level in dBFS is therefore the level in dBu minus that maximum, and headroom is the remaining distance to full scale.
  • The gain that puts a peak on the target is target + max input − mic output, and the source level that lands on the target with the gain you entered is the same expression rearranged.
  • The clipping ceiling is the loudest source that the input still accepts at the current gain. Lowering the gain by 6 dB raises that ceiling by 6 dB, and every 6 dB of extra source level doubles the voltage.
  • The page models levels only: it does not know the mic's self-noise or the preamp's equivalent input noise, so it cannot promise a signal-to-noise ratio, and it treats every figure as a peak. Converter alignment tolerance, pads or EQ placed before the preamp, input impedance loading and any loudness or audibility judgement are outside it. Nothing is measured or recorded — the arithmetic follows from the numbers you type.
