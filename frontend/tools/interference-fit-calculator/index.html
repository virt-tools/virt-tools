The hub is treated as a thick-walled cylinder under internal pressure and the shaft as one under external pressure, and the elastic deflections of the two parts must add up to the diametral interference δ. Lamé's solution gives the contact pressure p = δ / { d [ ((D²+d²)/(D²−d²) + νhub)/Ehub + ((d²+di²)/(d²−di²) − νshaft)/Eshaft ] }, with d the fit diameter, D the hub outside diameter and di the shaft bore. The hub carries a tensile hoop stress p(D²+d²)/(D²−d²) at its bore and 2pd²/(D²−d²) at its outside, and the shaft a compressive hoop stress at the interface. Friction over the contact area carries an axial force F = μpπdL and a torque T = μpπd²L/2; the axial figure is also the force a press must apply, because once the parts are seated the pressure comes from the interference and not from the press. Heating only the hub opens the bore by αdΔT, so the temperature rise is δ/(αd) for the interference alone and (δ + 0.02 mm)/(αd) with a 20 µm slip clearance. The safety factor divides the hub yield strength by the hub bore von Mises stress. This is a linear-elastic, plane-stress estimate of a single-step cylindrical fit: it ignores surface roughness and taper, centrifugal growth and service temperature differences, edge stress concentrations, fretting and fatigue, and the plastic redistribution that happens once the bore yields, so it is a preliminary sizing check rather than a design review.
