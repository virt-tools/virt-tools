Altitude Oxygen Calculator

See how much oxygen is in the air at an elevation: the standard-atmosphere pressure, the inspired oxygen pressure your lungs receive, and the share of the sea-level value.

Leave the pressure blank to use the International Standard Atmosphere for the elevation. Enter a station pressure from a barometer or an aviation weather report to use the real air instead.

      Inspired oxygen is the partial pressure of oxygen in air saturated with water vapour at body temperature, FiO₂ × (p − 6.266 kPa), not the oxygen that reaches the blood. The standard atmosphere is a reference model: real pressure moves by a few percent with weather, and temperature and humidity shift it further, so a measured pressure is more accurate when you have one. The numbers are physics, not medical advice; they do not predict altitude illness, acclimatisation or performance, and they say nothing about an individual's health.
