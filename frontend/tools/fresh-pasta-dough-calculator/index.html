How the batch is worked out

The dough is flour plus egg, and the egg-to-flour ratio says how much egg goes with each 100 g of flour: at 50 % a 50 g egg goes with 100 g of flour, which is the classic one-egg-to-100-grams starting point, and 45 % gives a drier, more semolina-like sheet while 60 % gives a rich yolk-heavy dough. Flour is then the egg weight times 100 divided by the ratio, and the dough is the two together.

Eggs cannot be split, so the count is always rounded up to the next whole egg. When that happens the flour is recalculated for the eggs you will actually crack, so the ratio stays exact and the batch comes out a little larger than the portions you asked for; the tool says how much larger rather than quietly shorting the dough.

This works out quantities only: cooking, drying, rolling and storing are outside its scope, and it is not food-safety guidance. Weigh the flour, crack the eggs into the well, and rest the dough covered for about 30 minutes before rolling. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is stored and nothing is sent.
