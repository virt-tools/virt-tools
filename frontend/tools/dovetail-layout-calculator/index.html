Enter the board width, tail count and ratio to see the layout.

A half pin sits at each end of the board, then tails and pins alternate, so a board with n tails carries n tails, n−1 full pins and two half pins. The pins and tails together fill the width exactly. Marks are measured from the left edge along the end grain and are shown to two decimals, from widths rounded to 0.001 mm, so a long run of marks can drift by a few hundredths of a millimetre. Mark the tails on one board and use the same layout, viewed from the other face, for the pins. Sides are usually cut at about 1:6 in softwood and 1:8 in hardwood. Saw kerf, board thickness and any deliberate asymmetry are not modelled, so cut a test joint in scrap first. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
