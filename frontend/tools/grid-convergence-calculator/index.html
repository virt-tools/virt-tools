Enter a position, a zone and a bearing to see the convergence.

Grid convergence is the angle at your position between true north and grid north, where grid north is parallel to the central meridian of the UTM zone. It is positive when grid north lies east of true north, so true azimuth = grid azimuth + convergence and grid azimuth = true azimuth − convergence. Convergence is zero on the central meridian and at the equator, and grows with the distance from the central meridian and with latitude. It is not magnetic declination: apply that separately for a compass bearing. The value uses a transverse Mercator series on the WGS84 ellipsoid; within ±3° of the central meridian it matches a full projection to about 0.01″, and out to ±6° to about 0.1″. Beyond 6° the page refuses because the series is no longer that accurate. This is a mapping and planning aid, not survey control or a navigation system. Nothing is stored and the page makes no network requests.
