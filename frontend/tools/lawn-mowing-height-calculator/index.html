Lawn Mowing Height Calculator

Grass that has grown tall after rain, a holiday or a broken mower cannot be fixed in one cut: taking off more than a third of the blade at once stresses the turf and lets weeds into the gaps. Measure from the soil surface to the top of the blades, not to the seed heads. Mow dry grass with a sharp blade, and check the deck height on a hard surface before the first pass. Most cool-season home lawns are kept between 2.5 and 3.5 in (6.4 to 8.9 cm). The page makes no network requests and stores nothing.

How the calculation works

Extension turf guides state the rule the same way: never remove more than one-third of the leaf tissue in a single mowing, and cut a lawn kept at 3 inches when it reaches 4.5 inches. This page applies that rule to height, pass by pass.

  • One pass. A cut to the target is legal when the target is at least two-thirds of the height before the cut. From 4.5 in down to 3 in is exactly one-third removed; from 6 in down to 3 in would be half the blade, too much at once.
  • Minimum passes. Each pass can leave at most two-thirds of the height it started with, so the number of passes is the smallest n with (2/3)n × current ≤ target.
  • Cut heights. The greedy plan cuts as deep as the rule allows every time: pass k leaves max(target, (2/3)k × current), and the last pass lands on the target. A 6 in lawn cut to 3 in takes two passes, 6 to 4 to 3.
  • Between passes. Give the grass a few days to recover before the next cut; mowing it all at once is what scalps it. Cutting direction can alternate to keep the turf upright.
  • Afterwards. Mow again when the grass reaches 1.5 times the target, which is 4.5 in for a 3 in lawn. Raise the deck an inch in midsummer heat if the lawn is stressed.

This is arithmetic for the one-third rule, not turf diagnosis. Species, shade, season, fertilizer and irrigation all change the height a lawn tolerates, and a lawn that is thinning, diseased or newly seeded needs different treatment.
