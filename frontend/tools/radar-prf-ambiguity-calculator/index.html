The pulse repetition interval is 1 / PRF, and a pulse may only listen for echoes that arrive before the next pulse leaves, so the leading-edge unambiguous range is c × interval / 2. Requiring the whole echo also gives the shorter full echo range, c × (interval − pulse width) / 2. Range resolution and the near blind range are both c × pulse width / 2, because a target closer than half the pulse length is hidden by the transmit pulse and two targets closer than that merge into one for a simple pulse. Velocity comes from the Doppler shift fd = 2v / λ: with the direction of travel unknown it folds at half the PRF, so the unambiguous velocity is ±λ × PRF / 4, and a moving-target canceller is blind at λ × PRF / 2 and every multiple of it. Duty cycle is pulse width / interval, average power is peak power × duty cycle, and the range bins per interval are interval / pulse width. The last row is the catch: unambiguous range times unambiguous velocity is c² / (8 × carrier frequency), fixed by the carrier alone, so raising PRF buys velocity at the cost of range and no PRF choice escapes it. This is simple-pulse timing arithmetic: it does not model pulse compression, coded or stepped-frequency waveforms, stagger or multiple PRFs, atmospheric attenuation, clutter, or detection probability, and it treats velocity as radial.
