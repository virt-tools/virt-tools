Parallax is the horizontal separation of the left and right images of a point, measured at the sensor. The focal length is first converted to pixels, focal ÷ sensor width × horizontal resolution, and the separation is that pixel focal length times the interaxial in metres times (1 ÷ subject distance − 1 ÷ convergence distance), so a subject at the convergence distance lands exactly on the screen plane. A subject nearer than the convergence distance shows crossed parallax and appears in front of the screen; a subject farther away shows uncrossed parallax and appears behind it. The budget is a share of the frame width; the near and far limits are the distances at which the separation reaches it, and the largest interaxial is the widest baseline that still keeps this subject inside it. Focal lengths of 4–300 mm, sensor widths of 3–70 mm, resolutions of 320–8192 pixels, interaxials of 0–500 mm, distances of 0.3–200 m for convergence and 0.2–500 m for the subject, and budgets of 0.05–5 percent are accepted. Toe-in, lens distortion, rolling shutter, floating windows and screen size are not modelled, and a comfortable parallax budget depends on projection size and viewing distance. Everything runs locally in your browser with no storage, uploads or network access.
