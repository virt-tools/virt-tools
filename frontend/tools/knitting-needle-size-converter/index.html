    How the sizes line up

    • Metric is the size. The millimetre value is the actual shaft diameter, so it is the only figure that means the same thing on every packet.
    • US numbers run up, old UK numbers run down. US 8, old UK 6 and 5 mm are the same needle. US sizing stops being used above 10 mm, and old UK numbering stopped at 8 mm; the larger millimetre sizes have no US or UK number.
    • Crochet hooks use their own labels. The letter and number in a label like H/8 repeat the same diameter series as needles, so a 5 mm hook and a 5 mm needle are the same gauge even though one is called H/8 and the other US 8.
    • Ranges, not rows. A few millimetre sizes have no number in one system — 3 mm has no US number, 3.5 mm has no old UK number — and makers disagree at the thick end (US 17 is sold as 12 mm or 12.75 mm). If a pattern names a size that is not in the chart, the nearest standard sizes above and below are shown.
    • This chart is a conversion table, not gauge advice: the yarn and your tension decide the fabric. Everything runs in your browser with no storage, uploads or network requests.
