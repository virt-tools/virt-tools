SSD Endurance & Write Budget Calculator

Turn an SSD's endurance rating into a write budget, and see how long the drive lasts at your daily host writes.

TBW is the total host data the drive is rated for; DWPD is how many times the whole drive is written each day over that period. Capacity is decimal, so 1 TB = 1,000 GB.

Vendors state endurance either as TBW, the bytes written over the rated period, or as DWPD, the number of full-drive writes per day over the same period. The two are the same figure: TBW = DWPD x capacity x 365 x years, with capacity in decimal terabytes, so an 800 GB drive rated at 10 DWPD for five years carries 14,600 TBW. TBW counts host writes at the workload the rating was measured at; the drive's own write amplification and over-provisioning are already inside the vendor's number, which is why a heavier random workload can use up the rating faster than the arithmetic suggests. The projected life here is linear: it divides the rating by the daily host writes you enter and assumes that rate never changes. S.M.A.R.T. data units written can be compared with the rating to see how much of it a drive in service has used. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
