Data Center PUE Calculator

Divide the energy the whole facility draws by the energy the IT equipment draws, and see what the overhead is and what a target PUE would ask of the same load.

Use the same window for both figures, a month or a year. The IT figure is what the servers, storage and network gear draw; the facility figure adds cooling, UPS and distribution losses and lighting.

A PUE of 1.4 means 0.4 units of overhead for every unit of IT load. The Green Grid's paper says 1.6 or better should be achievable with proper design.

Power usage effectiveness is the total energy the facility draws divided by the energy the IT equipment draws, so it is 1.0 for a facility with no overhead at all and rises as cooling, power distribution and lighting take a larger share; it can never fall below 1.0, because the facility total includes the IT load. Its inverse, data center infrastructure efficiency, is the IT share as a percentage. The Green Grid defines PUE as an energy metric and recommends reporting it as energy over a period rather than as an instantaneous power reading, because the ratio moves with the weather, the IT load and the way the plant is run; average power readings taken over the same window give the same ratio, since the window cancels. The paper sets out three measurement levels, L1 to L3, which differ in where the meters sit, so a PUE measured one way is not directly comparable with one measured another way; it also notes that many data centers run at 3.0 or worse while 1.6 or better should be achievable with proper design. PUE describes the infrastructure, not how much useful work the IT equipment does. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is sent or stored.
